# Chapter 17 — Drafting Brief

> The primary Phase 4 writer-facing document. Read alongside `canon-constraints.md` and `scene-breakdown.md`. This brief translates approved architecture into execution instructions; it introduces no new story decisions.

## POV Discipline

Aurelia, close third person, past tense, throughout.

Full Aurelia interiority is expected and necessary — her competitive read of the rankings in Scene 1, her search for a hidden catch in Voss's offer in Scene 2, her own physical reasoning at the stone line in Scene 4, her unnamed reaction to Aarav's admission, and her honest deliberation in Scene 5, are all required interiority beats.

Everything about Aarav, Voss, Dev, and Lyra must be inferred through their speech, physical action, and behavior, filtered through Aurelia's interpretation. No Aarav interiority. No Voss interiority. No Dev interiority.

**Aurelia's internal-movement axis this chapter:** competitive dissatisfaction → recognition of a genuine, guilt-free exit → engagement with hedged evidence → independent physical discovery → unnamed emotional awareness → honest deliberation → deliberate, costed choice. Not: doubt → vindication. If a drafted passage reads as Aurelia being proven right about anything, it belongs to the wrong axis — cut or rewrite it.

## Narrative Texture

The chapter should feel:
- inhabited — real rankings, a real evaluation deadline, a real institutional offer, before the mystery reasserts itself,
- personally weighted — the tension is *what does staying cost her*, not *what does the evidence mean*,
- restrained at the evidentiary level — Aarav's hypothesis is a hedge, not a discovery announcement,
- quiet and concrete at its ending — no ominous emphasis anywhere in Scene 6.

Avoid melodrama, heroics, villainy, and any atmosphere-over-evidence substitution.

## Scene 1 Execution — The List

Open on the posted rankings, not on any framing device. Let Aurelia's arithmetic (third place, two behind Lyra, eleven days to the evaluation) do the emotional work before Dev arrives. Lyra's line should land as genuinely ambiguous — not quite a jab, not quite sympathy — and she should be gone from the scene almost as soon as she's in it. Dev's drainage complaint must read as an authentic, long-running grievance he'd have voiced with or without Aurelia there; let him talk past her once or twice rather than delivering the regrading fact efficiently. Aurelia should set the detail aside deliberately, not forget it — the reader should sense it's filed, not dropped.

## Scene 2 Execution — The Easy Way Out

Keep Voss's office exactly as established: unremarkable by design, nothing that invites a second look. He should not perform reluctance — the offer is more convincing for arriving plainly. Give Aurelia room to look for the catch and not find one; her suspicion should read as habit, not evidence that Voss is lying. His remark that she's given the work "more of herself than he asked for" should land as genuine, not flattering. She must leave without answering, and the prose should make clear that not answering is itself a kind of answer, without over-explaining why.

## Aurelia's Choice — The Single-Decision Rule

This is the single most important discipline in the chapter, mirroring Chapter 16's non-vindication rule. Nothing before Scene 6 — not Scene 4's observation, not Aarav's admission, not Scene 5's arithmetic — may resolve into a stated or clearly implied decision. Every sentence in Scenes 4 and 5 that edges toward "and so she decided" must be rewritten as inclination or deliberation instead. When drafting Scene 5's close, actively check for language that settles the question early; if a sentence reads as the decision already made, it belongs in Scene 6 or must be softened back into "considering."

## Scene 3 Execution — Old Filing

Ground this scene in Aarav's own transcribed notes, not the index itself — he has access to structure, not incident content, per the exact grant Voss made at the end of Chapter 16. His explanation should proceed in the order: how the old system organized cases (by resolution status, not subject) → the explicit statement that the fourth stone appears nowhere in it → the hedge ("plausible," never "proven") that SERPENTFIRE's damaged fragment might have passed through that same structure. His distinction from Voss's Chapter 14 corridor theory must arrive as his own unprompted correction of the comparison, in his own words, before Aurelia can raise it — write it as him closing off the wrong reading, not as the narration doing it for him. Let Aurelia press him for precision ("plausible, not proven") rather than accepting the hypothesis at face value. Her realization about the regrading deadline should arrive as her own contribution, not something Aarav already knew — this scene works because each of them supplies half of what makes it urgent.

## Scene 4 Execution — What the Light Finds

This is the chapter's other central engineering challenge, alongside the single-decision rule. Slow down here. Have Aurelia walk the full line of eight stones in order before she reaches the fourth — the comparison only means something if the reader has felt the previous seven as unremarkable first. The observation itself must stay comparative and physical: wear, edge, age relative to its neighbors — never a claim about cause. Calling the flame should read as habit and verification, not expectation of an answer; let it fail to resolve anything, exactly as in Chapter 11. Write Aarav's confirmation through his own touch, not his agreement with her — he should be checking the same physical fact, not validating her conclusion. Place the romance beat only after the observation is complete: Aarav's admission should arrive unprompted, in the low, late-day light, without either character orchestrating proximity to produce it. Keep it to a few lines. Let Aurelia's internal reaction stay physical and specific to the moment (noticing something in him she hasn't seen) without naming the feeling, and return to the physical task — standing, brushing off knees, looking back down the line — within the same scene, not carried into Scene 5.

## Scene 5 Execution — What It Would Cost

Keep this scene moving — walking, not standing still to talk. Aurelia's arithmetic should echo Scene 1's rhythm (the same rankings-board logic, applied to the same eleven days) without repeating its wording. Dev's appearance should be brief and genuinely funny — his own self-aware humor about his consequence, not a guilt-trip aimed at Aurelia. Let Aurelia's reaction to him be quiet and specific (something settling that "wasn't quite guilt") rather than an extended moral reckoning. End the scene on her explicit refusal to let herself decide by default — she must tell herself, in as many words, that she hasn't decided yet.

## Scene 6 Execution — The Choice

This is the shortest scene and should feel like it. Aurelia speaks first, before Voss can ask anything, stating the decision plainly and without elaboration. Voss's reaction should cost him something visible and small — not surprise, but the quiet, specific disappointment of goodwill spent on an offer that didn't land — without any hint that this proves her right. His closing instruction ("bring Aarav tomorrow") should return the chapter to ordinary institutional rhythm rather than close on emotional weight. The final beat belongs at the rankings board: the number is unchanged; what's changed is what it costs her to look at it. Do not add a sentence after that final image.

## Relationship

Stage 2, edge of Stage 3 — earned but unsettled Respect, advancing toward, but not completing, the first demonstrated act of Trust. The new specific content this chapter adds: Aurelia acting on Aarav's evidence and her own extension of it, without either of them declaring what that means. Exactly one romance-coded beat, in Scene 4, unnamed by either character, never repeated or referenced again in the chapter. No friendship declaration, no romance-relationship language, no emotional-intimacy escalation beyond the single admission. No engineered physical proximity — Scene 4's closeness must be a direct, motivated consequence of comparing the stones by touch, not staged for the beat's sake; do not reintroduce the retired Chapters 11–15 shared-light/proximity device (see `chapter-16-continuity-input.md`).

## Mystery Handling

Two threads touch for the first time this chapter: SERPENTFIRE and the Chapter 11 fourth stone, through Aarav's hedged, administrative/historical hypothesis. Preserve total separation from the Angular Mark, the Unknown Student, the Chapter 13 annex marks, and the Chapter 16 "Outer Dark" heading — none should be mentioned, referenced, or gestured at. The hypothesis's boundary (SERPENTFIRE and the fourth stone's general case-category only, never a cause, never extended to any other thread) must be legible on the page through Aarav's own dialogue, not left to inference.

## Chapter 16 Protection

No restaging of the classroom exchange, Voss's admission, or the index-opening. The bounded index access is used here exactly as granted — structure only, never incident content — and is referenced as an ongoing, already-authorized activity, not something newly negotiated this chapter.

## World Texture

Deliver the rankings board, Voss's office, the Training Grounds' regrading, and the practicum-hall/baths corridor through action — no description blocks. The Scene 4 field should carry the physical texture of an active worksite (roped-off ground, surveying stakes, churned earth) without turning into a setting-description passage.

## Chapter 14/15/16 Differentiation

Keep this chapter unmistakably different in mode from Chapters 14–16: no restricted room, no institutional-authority-reveals-clue architecture, no stationary evidence-cataloguing scene anywhere. Chapter 17 moves outdoors, through ordinary Academy spaces, with mystery advancement occurring through observation and inference (Scene 4) rather than a second round of exposition (Scenes 5–6).

## Ending Lock

The chapter's final beat is Aurelia's felt ownership of the cost she chose, anchored in the rankings-board image. No sentence after it. No reflective coda, no stated question, no atmosphere-only closing image, no new mystery content.

## Prose Watchlist

Carried forward from prior chapter audits and specific to this chapter's risks:
- "the way [subject][verb]" constructions,
- clustering of "already" (target: no more than 2 uses total, well separated),
- unnecessary "specific" as hedge/intensifier,
- trailing "which" constructions,
- cognitive-storage metaphors ("filed it away," "tucked away") — vary phrasing instead,
- "didn't answer," "couldn't know," "not yet," "something was wrong," "what this meant" as recurring patterns,
- Voss's established descriptive fingerprint — vary his presentation from Chapters 14–16 rather than reusing a fixed formulation,
- any dramatic-pause or ominous-emphasis construction around the fourth-stone observation or the final rankings-board image.

## MUST

1. Open on ordinary evaluation pressure at the rankings board — no mystery content in Scene 1.
2. Establish the Training Grounds regrading as mundane, unweighted texture before it becomes relevant.
3. Have Voss's Scene 2 offer be genuinely unconditional, with a same-day deadline.
4. Have Aarav present his hypothesis explicitly hedged, and explicitly distinguish it from Chapter 14's discredited intruder theory, in his own dialogue.
5. Have Aurelia — not Aarav — connect the regrading deadline to the fourth stone's location.
6. Have Aurelia examine all eight stones herself and generate the wear-mismatch observation in her own words.
7. Place exactly one romance-coded beat in Scene 4, arising from Aarav's unprompted admission.
8. Keep Scenes 4 and 5 limited to inclination and deliberation only.
9. Reserve the single definitive choice for Scene 6 alone.
10. Give the choice a concrete, non-institutional cost (evaluation time, Voss's goodwill, Dev's light consequence).
11. End the chapter on the rankings-board image, with both mystery threads still open.

## MUST NOT

1. Restage any part of Chapter 16's classroom exchange, admission, or index-opening.
2. Claim or imply the fourth stone has any historical documentation.
3. Claim or imply SERPENTFIRE's actual origin, age, or meaning.
4. Claim or imply a confirmed connection or common cause between SERPENTFIRE and the fourth stone.
5. Let Aarav's hypothesis read as a restoration of Chapter 14's discredited causal theory.
6. Let Aarav supply or lead Aurelia to the central physical insight.
7. Let the wear-pattern observation read as proof of replacement, tampering, or agency.
8. Give Dev any investigation-adjacent access, job, or privileged knowledge.
9. Let the regrading read as anything other than a mundane maintenance deadline.
10. Stage more than one romance-coded beat, or use engineered/shared-light/shoulder proximity for it.
11. Let Aurelia decide anything before Scene 6, or decide twice.
12. Use a declarative "I trust you" or equivalent.
13. Advance the relationship past the first demonstrated act of Trust, or introduce Friendship.
14. Touch, resolve, or connect the Angular Mark, the Unknown Student, the annex marks, or the Chapter 16 "Outer Dark" heading.
15. Introduce any new mystery object, symbol, person, or event.
16. Reopen general Archive access.
17. End the chapter on a question or an unresolved/atmosphere-only image.

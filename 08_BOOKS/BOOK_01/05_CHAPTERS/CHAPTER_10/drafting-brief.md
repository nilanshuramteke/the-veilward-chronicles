# Chapter 10 — Phase 4 Drafting Brief

Draft the complete manuscript for **Chapter 10 — The Missing Record**.

**POV:** Aarav
**Narrative:** Close third person, past tense

## Before Drafting

Read every file in this package plus: finalized Chapters 1–9; the Chapter 9 finalization record, continuity handoff, and state lock; Book 1 canon, character canon, Academy rules; the Chapter 10 Phase 1–3.2 planning history (this package is its translation, not a replacement for its constraints).

## 1. Drafting Objective

Make the reader feel the specific texture of an institution declining to answer a question it clearly could answer. The chapter should read as: legitimate access used for the first time → a real, hands-on search → a discovery that lands with real weight → a boundary hit at exactly the point where the discovery matters most → an authority figure closing that boundary without pretending it doesn't exist → a changed, harder condition to work under going forward. The reader should leave more certain that something is being protected and less certain what it is.

## 2. POV Voice

Aarav's established voice: analytical, precise, controlled, cautious, competitive, verification-first, attentive to inconsistencies. Do not invent new voice traits. His investigation of Voss in this chapter is an extension of his existing "notice what doesn't fit" method — applied to an authority figure's behavior for the first time, not a personality shift. His interiority stays close and disciplined; he distinguishes, internally, between what he can support and what he merely suspects.

## 3. Opening Instruction

Begin inside the Chapter 9 access, already active. No recap of Chapter 9. No repeated authorization scene, no re-explanation of how the arrangement came to exist. Open with the Archive session itself getting underway.

## 4. Scene-by-Scene Drafting Instructions

See `scene-breakdown.md` for the full five-scene architecture, including Must Happen / Must Not Happen per scene. Do not add, remove, merge, or reorder scenes without a new authorized calibration pass.

## 5. Historical Record Writing Rules

The old record must be:
- generic — a magical irregularity described in flat institutional language, not a specific sensory event;
- procedural — written the way an ordinary incident report is written, not as atmosphere;
- believable — grounded, unremarkable in tone, exactly what a bored records clerk decades ago would have logged;
- incomplete — it stops at a supplement reference, not mid-sentence, not torn, not damaged;
- meaningfully parallel only through the ward contradiction — the fact that the ward check produced no accounting is the one thread connecting it to the present.

**Explicitly forbidden in this record:** cold; silence; sound disruption; voices, fragments, or speech of any kind. Do not let any sensory detail drift toward Chapter 4's signature.

## 6. Archive Writing Rules

Keep the Archive physical and believable, not systemic. Avoid: database or information-system language; technical retrieval terminology; extended shelving/catalogue description; blow-by-blow procedural narration of how requests are processed. The reader should experience the research through Aarav's attention and action — what he notices, what he checks twice, what he sets aside — not through an explanation of how the Archive works. The Archivist is present as a person doing a job, not as a system interface.

## 7. Voss Writing Rules

Voss is an institutional boundary in this chapter, not a lore source. His presence should read as controlled and economical — a handful of exact statements, not a scene of dialogue that drifts toward explanation. He can confirm the file's restriction, the supplement's existence, and his own authority. He cannot explain why, cannot describe cause, cannot gesture at deeper history. If a line of his dialogue would still make sense with the current mystery entirely removed from the story — i.e., it's about the fact of the restriction, not its content — it's safe. If it wouldn't, cut it.

## 8. Aurelia/Aarav Interaction

Maintain Stage 2 throughout. Their differences in method (Aarav's caution, Aurelia's directness) should emerge through what each of them does — how they search, what they're willing to ask for, how fast they push — rather than through another round of matched, symmetrical argument. No new debate scene is needed beyond what's already scoped in `scene-breakdown.md`.

## 9. Scene 5 Rule

Scene 5 is compressed by design. It must not become a debrief, a recap of the discovery, a philosophical exchange about what it all means, or an emotional-bonding beat. Its job is: brief aftermath, brief Academy-life texture, Aarav's private and unvoiced internal comparison (never spoken, never inferable by Aurelia), and a short, practical, forward-facing closing beat — not an open argument.

## 10. Ending Rule

The chapter ends with a changed investigative/institutional condition — Voss's reassertion of personal supervision — not with a new supernatural event, a cliffhanger, or another unanswered-question shrug.

## 11. Drafting Prohibitions

All items from `canon-constraints.md` §§4–10 apply without exception: no confirmed identity between old and current disturbance; no cause; no protected lore (Serpentfire, Elyra, Varun, Unknown Student, false intruder theory, hidden chamber, First Ember, any Echo agency/identity/origin/motive, Seeker, Outer Dark, Lost Path, Veil origin/weakness); no angular mark; no Chapter 3 wind reference; no relationship movement past Stage 2; no new Academy administrative system; no transfer of Aarav's Chapter 8 knowledge to Aurelia; no magical record restoration.

## 12. Prose Discipline

Carry forward all known recurring risks, confirmed across every prior chapter's audits:
- repeated "the way [subject][verb]" constructions;
- trailing "which..." clauses;
- "the kind of...that" constructions;
- excessive "already";
- cataloguing/shelving-register prose (elevated risk this chapter given the literal archive setting);
- over-choreography of archive/retrieval procedure (elevated risk in Scene 1);
- a static, purely-conversational feel in the discovery scene (elevated risk in Scene 3 — counter with physical business: handling the file, re-checking the stamp date);
- lore-dump dialogue (elevated risk in Scene 4 — every Voss line must pass the dialogue test in §7 above);
- symmetrical dialogue volleys;
- repeated "didn't have a clean answer" or equivalent formulations.

These are drafting warnings, not instructions to mechanically avoid natural grammar — they exist to prevent the specific patterns that have recurred across Chapters 4, 6, 7, 8, and 9's audits.

## Output

Return the complete Chapter 10 manuscript only. Do not include analysis, planning commentary, scene explanations, or audit notes with the manuscript.

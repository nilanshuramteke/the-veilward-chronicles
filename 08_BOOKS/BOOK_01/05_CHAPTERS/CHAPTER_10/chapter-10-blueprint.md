# Chapter 10 — Blueprint

## Chapter Title
**The Missing Record**

## Chapter Role

Chapter 10 opens Act II-A's investigation-deepening phase (Chapters 9–14, "How is this possible?"), immediately following Chapter 9's institutional-consequence close of Act I's tail. It is the first chapter to use the Chapter 9 supervised-access arrangement and the first substantive use of the Archive as a Book 1 location.

- **Relative to Chapter 9:** uses the access Chapter 9 earned; does not renegotiate it.
- **Relative to Chapter 11 ("What Fire Notices"):** hands off an active investigation and an unresolved method disagreement; explicitly does not perform Chapter 11's "Respect begins" turning point.
- **Relative to Chapter 12 ("A Name Without Meaning"):** delivers Mystery Clue Map items 4–5 (records contain gaps; a previous disturbance was quietly classified) while leaving item 6 (the word Serpentfire surfacing in an archive reference) untouched for Chapter 12.
- **Relative to Chapter 14/15:** does not seed the false external-intruder evidence or the midpoint collapse; the old record supports precedent, not intrusion.

## Core Question

**Can Aarav and Aurelia get an institution's own record-keeping to admit something it has already decided not to say — without losing the fragile access that got them inside in the first place?**

## Core Engine

**Earned access → ordinary archival search → historical precedent → incomplete/restricted record → Voss boundary → changed investigative situation → Chapter 11.**

## Narrative Function

Chapter 10 makes the mystery's history concrete rather than explaining the mystery itself. It converts institutional secrecy from an assumption into a demonstrated fact: the current anomaly is not unprecedented, and someone once decided its precedent should be hard to find. It also gives Aarav his first authority-vs-evidence problem, extending his established verification method from physical/testimonial evidence to institutional behavior.

## Required Story Beats

1. Aarav POV, close third, past tense, opening inside the active Chapter 9 access — no re-authorization.
2. A genuine, physical, procedural archive search using an ordinary index/reference method.
3. Discovery of a prior, witnessed, procedurally logged disturbance whose ward check also produced no accounting.
4. The old record terminates at a supplement reference the current access does not cover.
5. A restricted-holdings notation shows the record was reclassified after its original filing — quiet classification, made concrete.
6. A request for the supplement that the on-site Archivist cannot authorize.
7. Voss's on-page arrival and refusal, confirmed only as enforcement of an existing boundary — no explanation of cause.
8. Voss exercises his Chapter-9-reserved right to personally supervise future sessions — the chapter's institutional state change.
9. A brief, compressed closing beat that restores Academy-life texture and hands Chapter 11 an active, unresolved situation.

## Forbidden Story Beats

- Any re-negotiation of the Chapter 9 access terms.
- Any confirmation that the old and current disturbances are the same phenomenon.
- Any reveal of cause, entity identity, or motive for either disturbance.
- Serpentfire, Elyra, Varun, the Unknown Student, the false intruder theory, the hidden chamber, the First Ember, Echo identity/origin/motive, the Seeker, the Outer Dark, the Lost Path, true Veil origin/weakness.
- Any relationship movement beyond Stage 2, or any explicit "respect" framing.
- Magical restoration or reconstruction of the missing record.
- The angular mark or the Chapter 3 wind.
- A new Academy department, program, certification, roster, or administrative system.
- Transfer of Aarav's Chapter 8 behavioral-mismatch knowledge to Aurelia.

## Escalation

Chapter 9's movement was earning access. Chapter 10's movement is discovering that access has a real historical edge — and that the edge is enforced by institutional secrecy, not mere incompleteness. This is a genuine escalation: the obstacle shifts from "we don't have permission" (Ch7–9) to "the Academy has already decided not to let this be found" (Ch10), which raises the stakes for every future archive/history-adjacent chapter without resolving anything prematurely.

## Structural Differentiation

- **Chapter 5:** mark discovery via passive/incidental observation — Chapter 10 is active, procedural search.
- **Chapter 6:** witness/mark evidence cataloguing as the primary engine — Chapter 10's engine is institutional withholding, not evidence comparison.
- **Chapter 7:** a long two-person evidence negotiation — Chapter 10 introduces the Archivist and Voss on-page, breaking the enclosed-pair pattern.
- **Chapter 8:** physical anomaly testing followed by institutional interruption — Chapter 10's institutional beat *is* the discovery itself, not a separate interruption of it.
- **Chapter 9:** restitution/access-negotiation as the central engine — Chapter 10 uses the access rather than negotiating it.

## Ending Function

The chapter ends with a concrete, changed institutional condition (Voss personally supervising future sessions) rather than another unanswered question standing alone. The investigation is confirmed to have real historical precedent and a confirmed, deliberate information boundary around it. Nothing about the anomaly itself is explained.

## Chapter 11 Bridge

Chapter 11 inherits: an active investigation under tightened, Voss-personal supervision; a confirmed-but-unexplained historical precedent; an unresolved method disagreement between Aurelia and Aarav about how to proceed; and full, untouched space for "Respect begins" to emerge from its own material. Chapter 10 does not perform that emotional movement.

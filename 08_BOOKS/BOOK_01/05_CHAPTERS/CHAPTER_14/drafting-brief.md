# Chapter 14 — Drafting Brief

> The primary Phase 4 writer-facing document. Read alongside `canon-constraints.md` and `scene-breakdown.md`. This brief translates approved architecture into execution instructions; it introduces no new story decisions.

## POV Discipline

Aarav, close third person, past tense, throughout.

Full Aarav interiority is expected and necessary — his discomfort at losing interpretive control (Scene 1), his temptation toward the corridor theory and his precise recognition of its gap (Scene 2), and his choice to act despite unresolved disagreement (Scene 4) are all required interiority beats.

Everything about Aurelia, Mira, and Voss must be inferred through their speech, physical action, and behavior, filtered through Aarav's interpretation. No Aurelia interiority. No Mira interiority. No Voss interiority.

## Narrative Texture

The chapter should feel:
- institutionally grounded — procedure and consequence, not lecture,
- evidentiary — the tension is in what evidence supports versus what people conclude from it,
- increasingly purposeful as it approaches the midpoint,
- character-driven rather than lore-driven,
- structurally distinct from Chapter 13 throughout, not only in Scene 4.

Avoid melodrama, generic ominous language, manufactured danger, and atmospheric mystery language substituting for actual evidence.

## Scene 1 Execution — Complete Disclosure

Render the disclosure as a real, difficult character action, not a recap for the reader. Aurelia and Aarav are choosing, in the moment of being compelled to testify, to finally give the coherent account they promised each other at the end of Chapter 13 — this should carry a beat of its own before Voss responds. Then render Voss's presentation of the corridor as a real institutional finding, arrived at through his own credible process (the review's mandate naturally widened into auditing old access points once it began investigating how an unauthorized presence went unnoticed). Give Aarav's discomfort at watching their evidence become someone else's explanation a specific, physical, restrained beat — not a philosophical aside, not distrust of authority in general, just the immediate experience of losing control over interpretation.

Do not compress this into a list of three clue-names followed by a corridor reveal. Do not turn it into an exposition dump about how Academy reviews work. Give it room proportional to its several functions, the same way Chapter 12's own comparably dense scene was successfully paced.

## Institutional Interpretation

Voss's theory must be presented as the review's genuine, reasonable current conclusion — competent, not convenient, not incompetent. He connects the corridor's disturbed seal to the three newly-disclosed threads as supporting data points. The corridor's slated sealing is real institutional intent, not a stalling device.

## Aarav's Blind Spot

This is the chapter's central characterization risk. His temptation must never read as gullibility. Ground it specifically: he has spent the book verifying raw evidence and testing Aurelia's claims (Chapter 11), never before an institutional authority's own conclusion, because Voss has given him no prior reason to doubt one. The corridor record is the first checkable, physical, dated evidence the whole investigation has produced — his desire to trust it comes from the same values that make him rigorous elsewhere, applied to unfamiliar territory. His recognition, in Scene 2, must be triggered by the exact moment Aurelia raises the directional question (passage proves nothing about origin) — he realizes he hadn't asked it himself. Write this as recalibration, not shame or collapse.

## Aurelia's Skepticism

Give her three concrete, memory-grounded objections, delivered in her own plain, direct voice: the archway figure's composure and timing (Chapter 12); the annex worksite's active state (Chapter 13); and the sharpest one — a disturbed seal proves passage, not point of origin. She does not know the theory is wrong. She is identifying a gap, not delivering a verdict. She should not be effortlessly right or thanked for being right — the disagreement stays open through the end of the chapter.

## Mira

Keep her scene strictly behavioral. She notices something is off in how Aarav is acting — short, distracted, impatient — and says so plainly, with real dry humor, without letting him wave it off. She must never reference, guess at, or stumble into anything about the corridor, the theory, or Aurelia's objection. If a line of dialogue would require her to know anything about the case, cut it. Her value is that she reads him from entirely outside the investigation.

## Scene 4 Verification Mode

This is not Chapter 13 again. Chapter 13 was covert: an incidental, unsupervised worksite opportunity, hidden from the institution. Chapter 14's corridor visit is overt: having just disclosed everything, Aarav and Aurelia's decision to verify the corridor themselves is undisguised — say it aloud, or set it in a context where nothing is being hidden. Avoid every trace of stealth-scene vocabulary: no sneaking, no hushed voices, no covert glances, no shadows to slip through. The tension is "are we right to test this claim," not "will we get caught."

## Relationship

Keep rivalry and cooperative friction intact through Scene 1. Scene 2's disagreement should be substantive and unresolved — neither character wins it. Scene 4's relationship movement must be carried entirely by Aarav's direct question and Aurelia's direct choice to go with him despite disagreeing — no proximity beat, no unnamed feeling, no recalibrated distance, no romantic subtext of any kind. This is a partnership beat, not a romance beat.

## Stakes

Keep both stakes concrete and load-bearing, not decorative: Aarav risks the institutional credibility built since Chapter 12 if he has pushed a dead-end theory too confidently; Aurelia risks subordinating her own doubted judgment by acting alongside a conclusion she doesn't accept. Let these actually shape their choices in Scenes 2 and 4, not just sit in the background.

## Mystery Handling

Preserve the five-layer separation throughout: objective evidence (the corridor and its disturbed seal) / institutional interpretation (the intruder theory) / Aarav's interpretation (initial over-trust) / Aurelia's interpretation (the directional gap) / what remains genuinely unknown (cause, entity, motive, direction, and any actual relationship among the four threads). Never let narration collapse these into one confirmed truth.

## Chapter 15 Protection

Absolutely no disclosure, sensory hint, or implication of what lies beyond the corridor door, the true cause of the disturbance, the true direction of passage, or any part of the "breach theory is false" midpoint. SERPENTFIRE, the Unknown Student, and all protected lore remain untouched.

## World Texture

Deliver institutional-review procedure and the corridor's physical, historical depth through action and consequence, not description blocks or explanation. No new Academy department, policy, or bureaucratic system.

## Chapter 13 Differentiation

Keep the first three scenes unmistakably different in mode from Chapter 13 — institutional, evidentiary, and behaviorally observational rather than physical-investigative. Scene 4's corridor visit must read as a deliberate, disclosed, single verification act, not a second version of Chapter 13's trespass.

## Ending Lock

The chapter's final line or beat is the sealed door opening. Nothing — no sight, sound, smell, temperature, air, light, voice, figure, motion, atmosphere, or emotional reaction attributable to what's beyond it — may follow. End there.

## Prose Watchlist

Carried forward from prior chapter audits and specific to this chapter's risks:
- catalogue-like recitation of the three evidence threads in Scene 1,
- "clean answer" phrasing given the chapter's explicit theme,
- cognitive-storage metaphors ("filed it away," "set aside," "stored it," "tucked away," "shelved it") especially around Aarav's Scene 2 discomfort,
- refusal-to-introspect constructions ("didn't examine why," etc.),
- repeated "the way [subject][verb]" constructions,
- mirrored/triple negation,
- trailing "which..." clauses,
- clustering of "already,"
- unnecessary "specific" as hedge/intensifier,
- symmetrical dialogue,
- abstract emotional labels without physical grounding,
- generic mystery language ("ominous," "strange," "impossible," "watching," "waiting," "alive"),
- stealth/infiltration vocabulary in Scene 4 (explicitly wrong for this chapter's overt mode),
- the proximity → unnamed feeling → refusal-to-name-it construction, at highest risk in Scene 4 given its physical closeness.

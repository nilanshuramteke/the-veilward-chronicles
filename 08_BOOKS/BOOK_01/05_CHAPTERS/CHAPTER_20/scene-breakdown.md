### 5. Five-Scene Execution Map

#### SCENE 1 — AFTER THE STATEMENT (300–450)

**Objective:** Aarav recognizes the difference between answering the question literally and answering the responsibility behind it.

**Entry state:**
- The evening the statements are submitted. The statement is out of his hands and unseen by the reader.
- Location: an established setting with an ordinary evening anchor. Recommended: the cloister rail, where the lanterns are lit (Ch18). **UNVERIFIED which spot fits best; choose from established places.** Excluded: the wall, the index room, the carrel, Voss's office, the east passage and stair.
- He is alone. Aurelia is not present.

**Beats (proportions, not quotas):**
1. **An ordinary action and a time anchor** (~25%). Open on an object or an action, without atmosphere.
2. **Voss is reading tonight** (~10%). The anchor for the next scene.
3. **Aarav's own Ch13 line returns, with its lead-in** (~25%). "The day they ask." Use the established wording exactly if quoted; do not paraphrase into a slogan.
4. **The realization** (~30%). His rule is disclosure on request, so the asker draws the boundary. Voss asked about the mark and the wall. He answered to the edge of that question and decided, without saying so, that the edge was the end. It stays unresolved and unresolved-feeling. He does not conclude that he was wrong.
5. **Optional, one clause** (~10%). His unspoken objection at the wall (Ch19) or his reaction to being named. Cut if it inflates the scene.

**Information:** none new.

**Dialogue intent:** none. Zero spoken lines.

**Physical staging:** stationary or a single small action. Not a walk. No lamp-lit proximity. No Aurelia.

**Exit state:** a doubt, not a conclusion. The next scene begins in Voss's office, after he has read.

**Forbidden drift:**
- An inventory of what he withheld. Do not name Fourteen-C, the Ch8 comparison, or the Ch18 material.
- A replay of the submission or a reconstruction of the statement line by line.
- A generic summons.
- Explaining Aarav's psychology at length.
- A walk-away or quiet-reflection ending.
- "Neither…"

#### SCENE 2 — VOSS'S ASSESSMENT (1,000–1,300)

**Objective:** Fulfill Voss's Ch19 promise on the page. Assessment, not interrogation.

**Entry state:**
- The next day, in **Voss's office** (established Ch9 and Ch17; not used in Ch19). Aurelia and Aarav are both present. Voss has finished reading both statements.
- The scene opens after the reading, with no performed reading and no knock-and-statement routine (D5).

**Beats (proportions, not quotas):**
1. **Voss opens from the record** (~15%). What the two documents are and that he has read them.
2. **What they establish** (~20%). The shared physical observations, the points of agreement, and where they differ. The joint record and the statements are different instruments (Ch19).
3. **What they do not establish** (~15%). The meaning of the Mark, the meaning of the wall's text and its damage, the identity of the phenomenon, any relationship to the Echo, and any mechanism. He does not name Elyra or Varun. Aurelia's Echo line is one reported, unverified account, kept under her own "could not verify" heading.
4. **The difference in threshold** (~15%). One statement went past the scope he set; one stopped where the question stopped. Neither is faulted for honesty. He does not know Aarav has more and does not guess.
5. **The institutional conclusion** (~20%). The quality of a record turns on the line between observation and interpretation and on whether the people responsible for judgment get what they need. This is the chapter's real answer, delivered as his stance and not a lecture. Aurelia's three-heading structure is the concrete example of a usable record.
6. **The extended rule and his own scope** (~10%). Substance only: a student does not get to decide alone that something is not worth bringing when it could affect institutional judgment. He acknowledges his Ch19 question ("the mark or the wall") contributed. **Exact wording is a Phase 3 voice decision.**
7. **Ordinary dismissal** (~5%).

**Information:** what the statements establish and do not; the reporting principle; one institutional consequence (the rule).

**Dialogue intent:** Voss states an assessment in a controlled, concise register. Aarav registers the gap between "I answered what was asked" and "I gave the responsible people enough." It remains uncomfortable and is not resolved into a lesson.

**Physical staging:** the two statements as objects; the office's plainness ("nothing in it invited a second look," Ch17). Baseline Voss cues, each at most once. Aarav is POV, so his own statement's stopping point is the pressure.

**Exit state:** Aarav is uneasy, Aurelia is neutral, the rule is stated. No dramatic revelation.

**Forbidden drift:**
- A seven-question run, or any Ch19 questioning pattern.
- Ch17's office structure (D5).
- Voss saying the names, asking about them, or any second unasked-question beat.
- Voss knowing about Fourteen-C, the Ch8 comparison or Ch18 experience.
- Any explanation of the Mark, the Echo, Serpentfire, the Outer Dark or Elyra/Varun.
- A lecture on the Academy's purpose; a reference to the Story Bible.
- A punishment, a new restriction beyond the conceptual extension of the rule, or an authoritarian register.
- Voss "just knowing" what Aarav withheld.
- The "isn't cleared" construction (Ch12).

#### SCENE 3 — CONSEQUENCE (600–800)

**Objective:** An ordinary-life contrast, then Aarav's voluntary admission of custody and silence.

**Entry state:**
- Later that day. Location: an established outdoor route that passes the Training Grounds' low stretch (fence, roped-off stakes, Ch17). **UNVERIFIED exact path; choose from established places.**
- Aarav and Aurelia together, after Voss.

**Beats (proportions, not quotas):**
1. **Out of the office into the ordinary** (~15%). Academy life continues around them.
2. **The fourth stone** (~10%). Two or three sentences. The stretch is roped off or work is under way (Ch17). No stopping, no examination, no flame, no touch, no completion, exposure or "nothing found." The stone's oddity is not mentioned.
3. **Aarav volunteers** (~30%). The Fourteen-C transcription is still in his pack and was never formally surfaced. Nobody asked. It is a disclosure of custody and silence, not of the document.
4. **Aurelia's response** (~30%). Practical: why did he keep carrying it? She owns her share. Voss gave them the opening in Ch16 and neither took it. She is neither prosecutor nor absolver.
5. **"What else have you decided not to say?"** (~15%). She reaches it without being told there is more.

**Information:** custody and silence, and her share.

**Dialogue intent:** admission, practical question, shared ownership, the bridge question.

**Physical staging:** movement through a route is minimal and incidental. Aarav's pack is an object; he does not open it in this scene.

**Exit state:** the bridge question is asked and unanswered on the page.

**Forbidden drift:**
- Aarav explaining what Fourteen-C says.
- Aurelia discovering, reading or reacting to the document as new.
- A betrayal beat or moral prosecution.
- The fourth stone turning into a subplot, a test, or a connection to the drawer, the Mark, the annex or the Ch4 event.
- Dev, unless he earns the space. **Dev's crew status is UNVERIFIED** (Ch17 shows Dev *reporting* the regrading, not that he is on the crew).
- Disclosing the Ch8 comparison here (it belongs to Scene 4).

#### SCENE 4 — WHAT WE DO ALONE (1,300–1,700)

**Objective:** The emotional center. Shared uncertainty over unilateral control of it.

**Entry state:**
- Later that day. An established outdoor space that is not the Ch18 fountain, not the Ch19 roofed walk, and not the stone stretch, with ordinary traffic. **UNVERIFIED which; choose from established places.** Stationary or brief movement.
- Aurelia has just asked what else.

**Beats (proportions, not quotas):**
1. **He answers** (~10%).
2. **The Ch8 comparison** (~25%). What he noticed: that the Ch18 sound had a family resemblance to the Ch8 imitation, and that he did not put it into words. His hedge is his own Ch18 position: a resemblance, not a theory. Express it without "That's not the same as…". He offers his uncertainty about what it means, and he does not claim the events are the same.
3. **Why he held it** (~15%). Uncertainty, fear of contaminating the record, the belief that caution was responsible. His Ch18 worry about "the first version" sticking is his reasoning. The pattern is older than Ch18 (Ch10: "Some things you carried until there was a use for them"). One acknowledgment at most.
4. **Aurelia's objection** (~25%). Not that he is uncertain, but that he decided she did not get to know that he did not know. She is comfortable with "I don't know." Her Ch18 line ("I'd rather be wrong out loud") may surface once and unstressed. It is never a slogan.
5. **Different fears** (~10%). His: a premature or contaminated record. Hers: deciding blind and being managed.
6. **His recognition** (~10%). Caution applied alone controlled what she could know. He does not become a convert to telling everyone everything.
7. **The joint decision** (~5%). Fourteen-C goes to Voss, together, tonight. The scene shifts into logistics.

**Information:** the Ch8 comparison, his reasoning, and the joint decision.

**Dialogue intent:** Aarav explains without defending into a win. Aurelia names what he decided for her. Both give each other room to be uncertain.

**Statement-matching:** it surfaces only through the situation: two people holding overlapping knowledge and reporting different portions. It is never named as the Ch7 phenomenon and never explained. No "This is just like the witnesses."

**Ch8 and D6 handling (both UNVERIFIED — PRESERVE AMBIGUITY):**
- Aarav frames the Ch8 event only as much as his comparison needs, and does not restage the voice scene.
- Design his account so it works whether or not Aurelia heard the Ch8 voice.
- Aurelia must not say she heard the Ch8 voice, did not hear it, or already knew his interpretation.
- She was present at the Ch18 event. Do not tell her it happened.
- No detail (register, address, "went to her first") that fixes what she perceived.

**Physical staging:** the conversation serves the disagreement. No romance-coded proximity. No "quiet parsing" at length.

**Exit state:** honest, tested, not hostile; a concrete joint decision.

**Forbidden drift:**
- Aurelia demanding certainty or saying "you were simply wrong."
- Aurelia turning absolute or prosecutorial.
- A slogan.
- Any claim about the Echo's identity, mechanism, motive or origin, or that the phenomena are the same.
- The Ch18 experience going to Voss.
- The private attention beat, disclosed or restaged.
- A romance-coded beat.
- "That's not the same as…" (0), and "Neither…" (see §10).
- A dramatic walk-away ending.

#### SCENE 5 — THE CHOICE (500–800)

**Objective:** The completed action. The Fourteen-C transcription physically changes hands.

**Entry state:**
- That evening. An established standing/transition location that is **not** Voss's office, the Ch19 wall, the index room, the carrel, the roofed walk, or the east passage and stair. Ch16's standing corridor conversation is precedent for Voss conducting business standing. **UNVERIFIED which corridor; choose from established places.**
- They approach Voss on their own initiative, together.

**Beats (proportions, not quotas):**
1. **They reach him** (~15%). Brief.
2. **Aarav states what they hold** (~25%). The Fourteen-C transcription of the legible portions, and that it is "consistent with" the reference from Ch10. Never "identical to." One or two lines, not a Ch15 recap.
3. **Aurelia's part** (~20%). She read each finished line back against the original. She is a co-actor. (Echo: in Ch19 Voss set "Aarav writes. Aurelia checks each line against the stone.")
4. **The admission** (~10%). They kept it between themselves, and how they came by it, minimally. The original was left in place. The cost is the admission itself.
5. **The handover** (~15%). The pages leave Aarav's pack and pass to Voss.
6. **Voss receives** (~10%). He understands what he is being given and that it was previously kept from him. He states, as responsibility and not a plan, that what it means is now his to decide.
7. **The final image** (~5%). The handover, complete.

**Information:** the Fourteen-C transcription and its hedge reach Voss.

**Dialogue intent:** a state-what-this-is exchange, an attestation, and Voss's acknowledgment of responsibility.

**Physical staging:** the pages, the pack, Voss's hands. Voss does not open, read, or turn a page.

**Exit state:** something has changed hands. The reader should feel "they can no longer pretend this is only theirs," not "I wonder what it says."

**Forbidden drift:**
- Voss discovering the reference (he knows it: Ch10).
- Voss confirming the transcription is the sealed supplement, explaining contents, or explaining the old disturbance.
- Any connection to the Mark, the Echo, or Elyra/Varun.
- Voss opening a ledger, reading, cross-referencing, ordering a visit to the original, or assigning research.
- A dramatic punishment.
- Aarav disclosing the Ch18 experience.
- Aarav's summary becoming a recap of Ch15.
- The ending: an ominous sound, a door, a supernatural event, a new clue, or "we'll investigate tomorrow."

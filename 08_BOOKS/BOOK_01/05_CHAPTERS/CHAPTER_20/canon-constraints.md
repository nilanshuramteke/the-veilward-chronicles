### 2. Locked Character States

#### Aarav

**Entering:**
- Analytical, verification-first, precise.
- His statement is submitted and scope-literal: this mark and this wall. It is never shown.
- The Fourteen-C transcription is in his pack, never surfaced. He carried the decision to leave it there without ever putting it to Aurelia.
- He holds the Ch8 comparison and the Ch18 resemblance, unspoken. He also holds an unspoken objection to Aurelia's sequence at the wall (Ch19: he "almost says something else and does not").
- His private Ch18 attention is a legitimate private thought. It is never restaged, disclosed, or used as evidence of dishonesty.
- He is bound by Ch16's "through me" rule.

**Function:**
- He recognizes that answering literally is not the same as giving the responsible people what they need.
- He volunteers custody, offers the Ch8 comparison, and states his reasoning without defending it into a win.
- He is not a chapter-winner, and not a convert to total transparency.

**Exit:** Fourteen-C is surrendered jointly. The Ch8 comparison is shared with Aurelia. His Ch18 experience is still private from Voss. He no longer decides for Aurelia, alone, what she gets to know.

#### Aurelia

**Entering:**
- Direct and decisive. Her statement is submitted, and it went past the letter of what Voss asked (the Echo line).
- She corrected her own earlier Mark omission in Ch19 and holds standing from it.
- She knows Fourteen-C fully and is a co-holder of its silence.
- She was present at the Ch18 second manifestation; what she perceived is unestablished (D6).
- She does not know Aarav's Ch8 comparison.

**Function:**
- She holds Aarav to the standard she set for herself: shared uncertainty over unilateral control of it.
- She is comfortable with "I don't know." Her objection is that he decided she did not get to know that he did not know.
- She owns her share of the original silence.
- She attests to the Fourteen-C transcription's integrity at the handover. She is a co-actor, not an escort.

**Exit:** She understands the Ch8 comparison, has stood beside him in correcting the omission, and remains uncertain about the mystery.

#### Voss

**On the page:**
- Controlled, procedural, credible, observant and non-sinister, in the flat register of Ch16 ("without heat").
- He speaks concisely and in complete sentences. He is not authoritarian and not omniscient. His extension of the Ch16 rule is stated as substance; exact wording is a Phase 3 voice decision.
- He acknowledges, lightly, that his own Ch19 scope ("the mark or the wall") contributed to the ambiguity, without excessive self-blame.
- He knows what both statements contain, what he saw at the wall, the Ch10 reference, and that the students found something beneath the corridor. He does not know about the Fourteen-C transcription until Scene 5, and he never learns Aarav's Ch18 experience.

**Author-level only (never narrated):** see D12.

**At the handover:**
- He does not confirm the transcription is the sealed supplement.
- He does not explain the contents or the old disturbance.
- He does not open a ledger, start research, order an expedition, or punish.
- He states, as responsibility and not a plan, that what to make of it is now his to decide.

**Exit:** He holds the Fourteen-C transcription and is responsible for deciding what its presence means institutionally.

- What the Ch4 phenomenon was; what the wards failed to register, or why.
- What the Angular Mark means, who made it, or how old it is.
- What the wall's text says or means; the cause or age of its damage.
- Who Elyra and Varun were, or their relationship, fate, era or role.
- What the Echo is, why it imitates voices, or whether the Ch4, Ch8 and Ch18 events are the same.

- What Aurelia perceived in the second manifestation.

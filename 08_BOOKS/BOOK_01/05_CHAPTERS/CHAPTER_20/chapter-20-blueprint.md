**Chapter function:**
1. Convert the accumulated investigation into disclosure, responsibility, trust and a joint act.
2. Fulfill Voss's Ch19 promise on the page.
3. Show that literal compliance can still be unilateral decision-making.
4. Move Aarav from private custody to shared disclosure without making him fully transparent.
5. Give Aurelia standing to hold Aarav to her own standard without becoming a prosecutor.
6. Give the reader one real answer at the institutional and character level (what Veilward asks of its students and why), not at the supernatural level.
7. Break the investigation loop.
8. Deepen trust through disclosure and disagreement without romance.
9. Hand Ch21 a changed situation, not a new mystery.

**Core promise:** Aarav answered what he understood he had been asked, and stopped. Voss reads both statements and says what they establish and what they do not. Aarav admits to Aurelia that the Fourteen-C transcription is still in his pack and was never surfaced. She asks what else he has decided not to say, and he gives her the Ch8 comparison he kept to himself. She holds him to deciding alone what she gets to know. Together they hand the Fourteen-C transcription to Voss. The mystery does not move; the people do.

**Engine:** private doubt → institutional assessment → voluntary admission → shared disclosure → joint action.

**Central question:** When does caution become unilateral control over information?

### 11. Reader Experience Contract

| After | Reader should think |
|---|---|
| **S1** | Aarav is not sure that answering exactly was enough. |
| **S2** | Voss has said clearly what the records show and what they don't, and why reporting fully is how the Academy protects people. |
| **S3** | Aarav has chosen to say something nobody made him say, and Aurelia shares the blame for the silence. |
| **S4** | They disagree honestly, and she is not asking him for certainty, only not to decide for her. |
| **S5** | They can no longer pretend this is only theirs. |

**The reader must not leave knowing** what Fourteen-C says beyond Ch15, what the Mark or the wall means, what the Echo is, or what Voss will do. The chapter must be intelligible without the Story Bible.

**Reader-sim debts addressed:** what Veilward is for (through Voss's stance); one real answer (institutional); higher personal stakes (the restricted status and the decision to surface it); a broken investigation loop; character over another clue. **Left for later:** mystery-level answers.

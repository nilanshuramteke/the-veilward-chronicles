### Fourteen-C — Option Analysis

**Option A — Aarav discloses Fourteen-C to Voss within Chapter 20.**
- *Characterization:* Shows Aarav actually changing behavior, not just talking about it — the strongest single proof that Scene 4's argument landed. Risk: could read as too fast/too tidy for one chapter if not earned carefully.
- *Continuity:* Fully consistent with everything established; requires only that Voss's reaction stay bounded (genuine surprise at contents, not omniscience).
- *Mystery effect:* Advances institutional awareness (Voss now holds the material) without resolving what the disturbance was. Low risk of over-explaining.
- *Ch21 consequences:* Gives Ch21 a live institutional thread to inherit — what Voss does with it is Ch21's to decide, not manufactured here. Directly closes the loop Voss opened himself with "I won't ask... today," so it's earned rather than arbitrary.

**Option B — Aarav discloses to Aurelia but not yet to Voss.**
- *Characterization:* More gradual, arguably more psychologically realistic — trust rebuilt with her first, institutionally later.
- *Continuity:* Safe; no risk of misjudging Voss's reaction.
- *Mystery effect:* Keeps Fourteen-C fully private a while longer; no institutional advancement this chapter.
- *Ch21 consequences:* Weakens the chapter's own "concrete action" requirement — without an institutional beat, Scene 5 needs a different action to anchor it (see Scene 5 below for what that would have to be). Risks reading as one more version of "still didn't tell Voss," which the reader-sim already flags as fatigued, even though the flavor here is different (interpersonal, not evasive).

**Option C — A different piece of withheld information (the Ch18 second manifestation) is disclosed to Voss first; Fourteen-C stays fully deferred.**
- *Characterization:* Lets Aarav practice disclosure on lower-stakes material first — plausible as gradual growth.
- *Continuity:* Safe.
- *Mystery effect:* Nudges the Echo thread's institutional awareness slightly; leaves Fourteen-C's larger debt fully alive.
- *Ch21 consequences:* Keeps Fourteen-C as an open debt for a future chapter to spend — useful if the author wants more chapters of pressure before that disclosure lands, but produces a smaller, less visually concrete Scene 5 action than Option A (an admission is harder to stage as "action" than handing over a physical document).

> **AUTHOR DECISION — RECOMMENDED DEFAULT: Option A.** It produces the clearest concrete, physical, on-page action for Scene 5 (a document changing hands, not a conversation), it's the most directly earned by existing setup (Voss's own "not today" line), and it does the least damage to mystery pacing (no content is explained, only custody of a document changes). The plan below is built on this default but flags it as overridable — Options B/C are fully viable if the author wants Fourteen-C held longer.

## Scene 1 — After the Statement

**POV / Internal state:** Controlled, low-grade unease about one specific gap rather than a catalogued inventory. **Location / time:** That evening, shortly after last bell. A threshold space outside wherever statements were collected — not the index room (avoids restaging Ch19's reporting sequence).

**Participants:** Aarav alone.

**Opening beat:** The statement is already out of his hands. Physically past the point of revision.

**Scene engine:** A single, contained unease rather than an audit — he catches himself about to reach for the sheet to check something, and can't, because it's gone.

**Conflict:** Internal only — between "I answered what I was asked" and a nagging sense that the question itself was too narrow, without yet articulating why.

**Information revealed:** Nothing new about the mystery. The reader learns only that Aarav is uneasy, not what specifically he withheld — that surfaces gradually in Scenes 3–4, not all at once here.

**Character movement:** The first crack in his confidence that following the letter of an instruction is the same as fulfilling its purpose. Small — a felt discomfort, not yet a conclusion.

**Decision:** None. He resolves nothing here.

**Consequence:** Sets up Scene 2 emotionally — he arrives at Voss's assessment already slightly off-balance, not blindsided.

**Ending beat:** A summons for the following morning. Cuts on the summons, not on reflection.

## Scene 4 — What We Do Alone

**Purpose:** The chapter's central confrontation — authority vs. agency, argued through established character material.

**POV / Internal state:** Aarav, defensive at first, then genuinely affected when Aurelia turns her own established language on him.

**Location / time:** Late afternoon, walking — outdoors, in motion, deliberately not a lamp-lit or proximity-coded setting (explicitly prohibited).

**Participants:** Aarav, Aurelia only.

**Opening beat:** Aurelia demands the real answer to "what else" — starting with the second manifestation, since she can tell there's more.

**Scene engine:** Direct confrontation built from established material, not invented rhetoric.

**Conflict:**
- Aarav discloses the Ch18 second manifestation for the first time, using **observation → uncertainty → interpretation**, explicitly separated: what he sensed (the register, the reaching-and-missing, going to her first), flagged clearly as uncertain, and — only as his own admitted guess, never asserted as fact — that it might resemble the Ch8 Dev-voice mismatch. He does not claim to know what either phenomenon is, and nothing here confirms the Ch8 and Ch18 events are the same thing.
- Aurelia's objection is not "you should have told Voss" — she reuses her own Ch19 line: *"I'm not handing anyone half of anything."* Turned on him: he's been handing her half of everything.
- Aarav's defense is his real, established Ch18 reasoning (protecting an unformed account from becoming "the first version that sticks") — now turned toward her rather than Voss, which is the point: defensible institutionally, not defensible interpersonally.
- Aurelia's sharper counter: it was never his call alone to make about what she gets to know.

**Statement-matching — parallel, not explanation:** Aarav notices, mid- or post-argument, that he and Aurelia just demonstrated something structurally similar to the old Ch7 pattern: two people holding overlapping information and reporting different portions of it. He should explicitly catch himself before treating this as proof of anything — a line like *"That's not the same question. That doesn't explain why the accounts differed then"* — so the parallel is visible to the reader without becoming a quiet solution to a mystery nobody has actually solved. This is a character-level observation about disclosure, not a theory of the Ch7 phenomenon.

**Information revealed:** Why Aarav withheld (protecting an uncertain record); why Aurelia objects (agency, not correctness); the statement-matching parallel, explicitly bounded.

**Information withheld:** The attention beat — not raised here, and treated as a legitimate omission (see Design Decisions), not another evasion.

**Character movement:** Aarav moves from defending his reasoning to recognizing its cost, without conceding the caution itself was wrong — only that applying it unilaterally is itself a decision made *for* her. Aurelia moves from anger toward the Phase 1 target: not "you should have told Voss" but "you don't get to decide that for both of us." Neither fully wins.

**Decision:** They don't resolve the abstract question. They agree, tensely, that Fourteen-C specifically needs action tonight — the concrete question displacing the abstract one.

**Consequence:** Sets up Scene 5 directly.

**Ending beat:** A shift from argument to logistics — the walk changes direction.

**Canon locks:** No romance-coded beat, no confession, no physical intimacy. Established voice patterns reused, not invented. The Ch18/Ch8 resemblance stays a private, hedged guess — never confirmed.

**Mystery boundaries:** No resolution of the Echo's nature, no Ch7 explanation, no connection of Fourteen-C to Elyra/Varun.

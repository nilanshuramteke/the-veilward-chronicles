# Chapter 4 Reader Promise Check

## Why this check exists
The Chapter 1–3 reader simulation found strong voice, rivalry, and competence-based chemistry but identified the absence of a plot-level question by the end of Chapter 3.

Chapter 4 is the authorized structural response.

This is not a mandate to add retroactive foreshadowing to Chapters 1–3.

## Required reader experience
By the end of Chapter 4, a first-time reader should reasonably ask:

**What just happened, and how could it have happened inside Veilward?**

## Success criteria

### The event is concrete
The reader experiences an actual event, not merely characters discussing that something may be strange.

### The event changes the story
After Chapter 4, the book cannot simply return to three more chapters of ordinary rivalry and evaluation without addressing what happened.

### The question is specific enough
The mystery should have an observable contradiction:
- an impossible event occurred,
- but Veilward's defenses detected no intrusion.

### The answer remains withheld
The chapter must not solve the contradiction.

## Failure modes

### Too little
- vague unease,
- symbolic hints,
- unexplained ominous language,
- another ordinary evaluation chapter.

### Too much
- explaining the Echo,
- identifying its origin,
- naming deeper lore,
- revealing later mystery answers.

## Relationship check
The mystery event must not use danger as a shortcut to:
- romance,
- attraction,
- friendship,
- confession,
- or emotional intimacy.

Aurelia and Aarav remain rivals entering Chapter 5.

## Final question
After reading Chapter 4 alone, would a reader plausibly continue because the book now contains a concrete unanswered problem?

If no, Chapter 4 has not fulfilled its structural function.

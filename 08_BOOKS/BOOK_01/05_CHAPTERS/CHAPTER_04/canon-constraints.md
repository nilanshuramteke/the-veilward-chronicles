# Chapter 4 Canon Constraints

## Authority order
Use the established canon authority matrix and Book 1 canon locks.

When sources conflict or appear incomplete, do not invent a reconciliation inside the manuscript.

## POV
Aarav, close third person, past tense.

Aarav may:
- observe,
- infer,
- compare,
- test explanations,
- and revise conclusions.

He may not know another character's private thoughts.

Inference must remain inference unless the fact is directly observable or canon-established.

## Chapter 4 mystery authorization
Chapter 4 is authorized to introduce:
- cold,
- silence,
- voices that do not belong,
- the Echo's manifestation,
- and the fact that Veilward's defenses detect no intrusion.

This authorization does not permit explanation.

## Protected information
Do not reveal, name, explain, or strongly imply:
- the Echo's origin,
- the Echo's purpose,
- Outer Dark involvement,
- Serpentfire,
- the First Ember,
- erased history,
- ancient containment,
- the hidden chamber,
- historical predecessors,
- or later revelations scheduled for downstream chapters.

## Echo handling
The Echo may be observed.

Do not define:
- what it is,
- how it thinks,
- what it wants,
- how long it has existed,
- where it came from,
- or what rules govern it

unless directly supported by approved canon for this chapter.

Avoid explanatory narration disguised as observation.

Any procedural response around checking the defenses is provisional/local unless canon-established.

## Wind guardrail

Do not manufacture mystery through repeated ominous-negation constructions or vague prophecy-style language.

The impossible event must be disturbing because of what characters directly experience, not because narration announces that something ancient, watching, waiting, or wrong has awakened.

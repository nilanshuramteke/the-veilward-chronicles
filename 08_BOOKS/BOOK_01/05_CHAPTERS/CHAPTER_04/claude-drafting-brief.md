# Claude Drafting Brief — Chapter 4

## Chapter
**Chapter 4 — The Impossible Breach**

## Current status
Pre-drafting.

Do not draft the manuscript until:
1. Phase 1 orientation is complete.
2. Phase 2 detailed scene plan is produced.
3. Phase 3 review and revision are complete.
4. The scene plan is approved.

## Read before working
Read the complete Chapter 4 package and relevant upstream canon.

Minimum required context:
- Chapter 4 blueprint
- Chapter 4 scene breakdown
- Chapter 4 canon constraints
- Chapter 3 continuity input
- relevant Book 1 canon locks
- relevant magic usage lock
- Aarav character file
- Aurelia character file
- Lyra character file if Lyra is used
- finalized Chapters 1–3 when needed for continuity

## Phase 1 — Orientation
Confirm:
- POV,
- chapter purpose,
- entry state,
- required Chapter 4 events,
- protected mystery information,
- relationship boundaries,
- Chapter 3 wind restriction,
- and audit risks.

Do not draft scenes.

## Phase 2 — Detailed Scene Plan
Produce a scene-by-scene plan.

For every scene include:
1. Purpose
2. Starting situation
3. POV character objective
4. Internal state
5. Conflict
6. Major beats
7. Character function
8. Information revealed
9. Information withheld
10. Emotional movement
11. Transition

### Mandatory mechanic labeling
Every proposed procedural or evaluation-response mechanic must be labeled:
- **Canon-established**, or
- **Provisional/local**.

Do not present provisional mechanics as permanent Academy canon.

## Phase 3 — Scene Plan Review
Review the plan for:
- canon compliance,
- continuity,
- POV,
- mystery firewall,
- pacing,
- reader promise,
- relationship state,
- and accidental explanation.

Revise only where required.

## Phase 4 — Manuscript Draft
Only after scene-plan approval.

Draft:
- Aarav close third,
- past tense,
- analytical but human,
- observation before conclusion.

The anomaly must be experienced before it is explained or categorized.

## Phase 5 — Canon and Continuity Audit
Audit the complete manuscript.

Report issues with:
- exact passage,
- problem,
- relevant rule,
- severity,
- recommended correction.

Do not silently rewrite during an audit unless explicitly instructed.

## Phase 6 — Narrative Quality Audit
Use the 14-category framework plus:
- Aarav POV differentiation,
- mystery escalation,
- reader-promise fulfillment,
- repetition scan.

## Phase 7 — Corrections and Micro-Audits
Apply only authorized corrections.

After each correction pass:
- check immediate repetition,
- check new ambiguity,
- check canon integrity,
- check POV integrity.

## Chapter 4 non-negotiables

### Must happen
- training/evaluation environment changes,
- cold,
- silence,
- voices that do not belong,
- Echo manifestation,
- defenses detect no intrusion.

### Must not happen
- Echo explanation,
- Outer Dark reveal,
- Serpentfire,
- First Ember,
- ancient-history explanation,
- retroactive Chapter 3 wind foreshadowing,
- romance,
- friendship breakthrough,
- permanent competitive hierarchy.

## Writing-pattern watch
Avoid recurring:
- trailing `which...` clauses,
- `the way he/she...`,
- `the kind of...`,
- repeated sentence openings,
- overly symmetrical dialogue volleys,
- ominous negation constructions.

Do not solve repetition by introducing a new uniform substitute template.

## Core principle
**Observation is not explanation.**

The reader should leave Chapter 4 knowing that something happened and wanting to know how, not knowing what the anomaly actually is.

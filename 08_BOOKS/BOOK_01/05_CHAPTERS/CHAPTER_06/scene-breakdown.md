# Chapter 6 — Separate Accounts: Scene Breakdown

## Planning Status

This is a drafting-stage scene framework, not immutable prose canon.

Scene staging and minor mechanics are **Provisional/Local** unless established elsewhere. The chapter's structural outcomes are fixed by the Book One outline and continuity handoff.

---

## Scene 1 — The Accounts

### Purpose
Move immediately into the consequences of the Chapter 4 event and establish that witness comparison has become the current pressure point.

### Starting State
Aarav encounters the active process of gathering, reviewing, or comparing accounts of the incident.

### External Objective
Determine whether the witness reports contain a reconcilable ordinary pattern.

### Major Beats
- Aarav approaches the reports as data rather than mystery.
- Broad facts align: cold, sound disruption, voices, visible disturbance.
- Granular details do not.
- He tests ordinary explanations first: stress, sequence confusion, incomplete recall, suggestibility, or simple wording differences.
- The discrepancies remain after the obvious comparison problems are accounted for.

### Information Revealed
The event is shared, but manifestations are not described identically.

### Emotional Movement
Controlled focus → analytical resistance → unresolved irritation.

### Transition
A specific discrepancy points Aarav toward Aurelia's account.

---

## Scene 2 — Aurelia's Missing Detail

### Purpose
Establish that Aurelia perceived something Aarav did not.

### Starting State
Aarav compares her account against his own and against the broader witness record.

### External Objective
Clarify whether the discrepancy is wording, memory, or a genuinely distinct observation.

### Major Beats
- Aarav and Aurelia compare accounts directly or through a constrained institutional setting.
- Their exchange remains pointed and competitive.
- Aurelia does not deny the event or surrender her interpretation merely because Aarav prefers clean patterns.
- Aarav identifies one detail absent from his own experience.
- The detail is treated as a reported observation, not proof of what the anomaly is.

### Information Revealed
Aurelia has a piece of sensory or visual information Aarav did not perceive.

### Emotional Movement
Methodological friction → sharper attention → reluctant recognition of a real difference.

### Transition
The detail contains a factual resemblance Aarav recognizes.

---

## Scene 3 — The Mark in the Account

### Purpose
Bring the witness discrepancy into factual contact with the recurring angular mark without converting the mark into an answer.

### Starting State
Aarav recognizes that Aurelia's described detail resembles the three-stroke mark.

### External Objective
Determine whether the resemblance is actually the same observable pattern or only Aarav forcing a match.

### Major Beats
- Aarav asks Aurelia to describe the detail without leading her.
- She provides or reconstructs the detail from her own memory.
- Aarav compares it against the known mark.
- The factual overlap is strong enough to acknowledge.
- Aurelia challenges the significance Aarav may be tempted to assign to recurrence.
- Aarav does not claim meaning; he insists only that repeated matching facts are worth accounting for.

### Information Revealed
A detail Aurelia perceived during the event matches the established three-stroke angular mark closely enough to be treated as the same observed pattern.

### Explicit Boundary
This does **not** establish:
- that the mark caused the event,
- that the anomaly created the mark,
- that the mark is magical,
- that the event was communicating,
- that anyone or anything intended the pattern.

### Emotional Movement
Recognition → methodological disagreement → disciplined uncertainty.

### Transition
Both characters are left holding a stronger factual puzzle without agreement on what to do with it.

---

## Scene 4 — Separate Accounts

### Purpose
Land the chapter's movement: complementary evidence exists, but cooperation has not begun.

### Starting State
The comparison is complete enough to establish the factual problem.

### External Objective
Neither character has an authorized explanation or an agreed investigative path.

### Major Beats
- Aarav recognizes that his own account is incomplete, not necessarily incorrect.
- Aurelia refuses to let incompleteness become ownership of the problem.
- Their rivalry remains intact through disagreement about method or significance.
- No partnership is proposed.
- No tasks are divided.
- No emotional intimacy is created.
- The chapter ends on the practical contradiction: neither account contains the whole picture, and neither the wards nor the witnesses can reconcile what happened.

### Information Withheld
All explanation.

### Emotional Movement
Focused recognition → resistance to premature conclusion → unresolved forward pressure.

### Exit State
They possess complementary evidence.

They remain separate.

Chapter 7 inherits the pressure to force or authorize cooperation.

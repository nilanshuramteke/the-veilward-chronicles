# Chapter 6 — Separate Accounts: Blueprint

## 1. Chapter Function

Chapter 6 advances the mystery through contradiction rather than explanation.

The Chapter 4 event was objectively shared, but witness accounts do not agree on every detail. Aarav approaches those differences as evidence to compare, not proof of a theory. During that process, he learns that Aurelia perceived a detail absent from his own experience. That detail intersects with the recurring angular mark established in Chapter 5.

The chapter's structural job is to establish **complementary evidence** without allowing complementary evidence to become partnership.

## 2. Reader Promise

The reader should feel that the mystery is moving forward immediately after Chapter 5.

By the end, the reader should understand:

- the witnesses experienced the same event but not identical manifestations,
- Aarav and Aurelia each hold information the other does not,
- one newly compared detail creates a factual overlap with the recurring mark,
- none of those facts yet explain what happened.

Primary question carried forward:

**How can one objectively shared event produce incompatible details, and what should be made of the recurring mark without assuming it explains anything?**

## 3. POV and Character State

### POV
Aarav, close third person, past tense.

### Entering state
Aarav remains:
- precise,
- analytical,
- controlled,
- pattern-sensitive,
- inclined to test ordinary explanations before accepting unresolved ones.

He begins Chapter 6 knowing:
- the event happened,
- witness details differ,
- the ward record does not account for the event,
- the recurring mark exists in two locations,
- Aurelia independently found one of those marks.

He does **not** know what the anomaly is or what the mark means.

### Relationship state
Aurelia and Aarav remain:
- rivals,
- independently competent,
- not friends,
- not partners,
- without a shared investigative agreement.

## 4. Authorized Chapter Movement

Chapter 6 may:
- compare or collect witness accounts as an immediate consequence of the Chapter 4 event,
- reveal contradictions among granular witness details,
- allow Aarav to identify that Aurelia perceived something he did not,
- establish that a detail from Aurelia's account factually matches the already-established angular mark,
- create investigative pressure that Chapter 7 can convert into forced cooperation.

Chapter 6 must not:
- explain the anomaly,
- establish why witness perceptions differ,
- establish the mark's meaning or origin,
- claim the mark caused the event,
- make the mark magical,
- make Aurelia and Aarav partners.

## 5. Mystery Hierarchy

### Primary
A real shared event occurred, yet Veilward's records cannot account for it.

### Supporting pressure
Witness accounts disagree about specific manifestations despite agreement that the event occurred.

### Secondary clue
The recurring three-stroke angular mark now intersects with a detail Aurelia perceived during the event.

### Explicitly not established
- what the anomaly is,
- whether it is intelligent or aware,
- where it came from,
- why accounts differ,
- whether the mark is connected to the anomaly,
- what the mark means,
- who made it,
- whether it is magical.

## 6. Relationship Movement

The chapter does not create emotional closeness.

The movement is informational only:

**separate evidence → reluctant comparison → factual overlap → unresolved competitive tension**

A shared fact remains a shared fact, not a shared agenda.

## 7. Exit Pressure into Chapter 7

By the end:
- Aarav knows Aurelia perceived something he did not,
- Aurelia knows Aarav is treating the discrepancy as evidence worth accounting for,
- the mark has become a stronger factual point of curiosity through recurrence and account comparison,
- neither has authority to define its meaning,
- neither has agreed to investigate with the other.

This creates a legitimate foundation for Chapter 7's forced or controlled cooperation without prematurely starting that cooperation here.

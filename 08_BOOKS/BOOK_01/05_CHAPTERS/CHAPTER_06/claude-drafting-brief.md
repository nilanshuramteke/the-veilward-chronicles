# Chapter 6 Drafting Brief

## Assignment

Draft Chapter 6, **Separate Accounts**, using the Chapter 6 package as the controlling chapter-level authority and the established Book One canon as upstream authority.

POV: **Aarav, close third person, past tense.**

Do not draft beyond Chapter 6's authorized movement.

## Core Job

Advance the central mystery through conflicting witness accounts.

The chapter must establish:

1. The Chapter 4 event was objectively shared.
2. Witnesses disagree on granular details.
3. Aarav discovers Aurelia perceived something he did not.
4. One detail from Aurelia's account factually matches the recurring three-stroke angular mark.
5. Aarav and Aurelia therefore possess complementary evidence.
6. They do not become partners.

## Priority Hierarchy

### Primary mystery
A real event occurred, but Veilward's wards provide no accounting for it.

### Supporting complication
Witness accounts do not align on every detail.

### Secondary clue
The angular mark recurs and now intersects with a detail from Aurelia's account.

Do not allow the mark to replace the ward contradiction as the chapter's main mystery.

## Aarav Voice

Aarav should remain:
- precise,
- analytical,
- controlled,
- skeptical of easy conclusions,
- willing to test mundane explanations first.

Do not make him omniscient.

His analysis should be visible through:
- what he notices,
- what he tests,
- what he rejects,
- what he refuses to claim.

## Aurelia Voice in Aarav POV

Aarav may observe Aurelia's:
- directness,
- impatience,
- competence,
- resistance to tidy conclusions.

He may not know her private thoughts.

Do not flatten her into a delivery mechanism for Aarav's discovery.

Her account must remain independently hers.

## Mystery Discipline

Observation is not explanation.

Do not:
- identify the anomaly,
- assign intelligence,
- imply intention,
- imply awareness,
- explain the witness discrepancy,
- explain the mark,
- imply the mark caused the event,
- imply the anomaly produced the mark.

Avoid entity-like atmospheric language.

The phenomenon should remain compelling because facts fail to reconcile.

## Ward Language

Use only neutral continuity:

The ward record contains nothing capable of accounting for what witnesses experienced.

Do not write:
- breach,
- entry,
- crossing,
- escape,
- inside origin,
- outside origin,

as established explanations.

## Relationship Boundary

Do not create:
- partnership,
- friendship breakthrough,
- romantic intimacy,
- emotional confession,
- shared investigative plan.

Complementary evidence is not collaboration.

Chapter 7 owns the forced-cooperation transition.

## Reader Contract

Open on active mystery movement.

Do not begin with:
- drills,
- training,
- evaluation routine,
- ordinary Academy life,

before engaging with the witness-account problem.

## Prose Watch

Avoid known repetition patterns:
- trailing `which...`,
- `the way [he/she]...`,
- `the kind of...that`,
- disguised reduced-relative versions.

Do not replace them with a new mechanical template.

Avoid vague atmospheric claims such as:
- something felt wrong,
- the room was unnatural,
- an ancient presence,
- something watching or waiting.

Use concrete facts.

## Chapter Ending

End with:
- complementary evidence established,
- explanation withheld,
- rivalry intact,
- no partnership,
- clear pressure into Chapter 7.

Stop at the end of the chapter.

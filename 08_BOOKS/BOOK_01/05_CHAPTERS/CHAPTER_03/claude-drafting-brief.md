# Claude Drafting Brief — Chapter 3

## Mandatory workflow
Do not draft the manuscript immediately.

### Phase 1 — Read
Read every file in this package and the relevant higher-authority canon files referenced by it.

### Phase 2 — Scene Plan
Create a detailed scene plan before prose. For each scene provide:
1. Purpose
2. Starting situation
3. Aurelia's external objective
4. Internal state
5. Conflict
6. Major beats
7. Character function
8. Information revealed
9. Information withheld
10. Emotional movement
11. Transition

When proposing the direct-opposition exercise, explicitly label which mechanics are established canon and which are provisional local scene details.

### Phase 3 — Scene Plan Audit
Audit the plan for canon, POV, rivalry progression, Lyra's function, mystery protection, Academy-mechanics overreach, and repetition with Chapter 2.
Do not proceed until the plan is approved.

### Phase 4 — Draft
Draft only after approval. Close third person, past tense, through Aurelia.

### Phase 5 — Canon Audit
Audit the complete manuscript against the package and higher canon. Report exact passage, problem, rule, severity, and correction.

### Phase 6 — Narrative Quality Audit
Audit opening, pacing, scene purpose, emotional progression, characterization, dialogue, rivalry, exposition, description, sentence variety, repetition, artificial AI patterns, ending strength, and POV differentiation.

### Phase 7 — Corrections and Micro-Audit
Correct only authorized findings. Re-scan edited passages for newly introduced echoes before declaring the chapter ready for approval.

## Drafting guardrails
- Action and competition must come from concrete objectives, adjustments, and consequences, not constant banter.
- Do not manufacture suspense through ominous language.
- Do not invent permanent Academy rules.
- Do not give Aarav impossible knowledge.
- Keep Lyra specific and active, but do not use her as an exposition machine.
- Do not resolve who is better.

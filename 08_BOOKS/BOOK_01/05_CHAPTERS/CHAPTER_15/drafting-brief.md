# Chapter 15 — Drafting Brief

> The primary Phase 4 writer-facing document. Read alongside `canon-constraints.md` and `scene-breakdown.md`. This brief translates approved architecture into execution instructions; it introduces no new story decisions.

## POV Discipline

Aurelia, close third person, past tense, throughout.

Full Aurelia interiority is expected and necessary — her readiness with nowhere to land (Scene 1), her destabilization and embodied stopped-motion beat (Scene 4), and her quiet collaborative trust (Scene 5) are all required interiority beats.

Everything about Aarav must be inferred through his speech, physical action, and behavior, filtered through Aurelia's interpretation. No Aarav interiority.

## Narrative Texture

The chapter should feel:
- like a midpoint — evidence accumulating toward a genuine reversal, not another incremental clue,
- physically grounded — search, discovery, and transcription as concrete acts, not conversation about them,
- historically real — the annex feels like genuine institutional history, not a generic dungeon or a lore-dump opportunity,
- character-driven — the reversal lands through Aurelia's and Aarav's distinct reasoning, not authorial explanation.

Avoid melodrama, generic ominous language, manufactured danger, and atmospheric mystery language substituting for actual evidence.

## Scene 1 Execution — Threshold

Render the crossing itself briefly — this is not a new mystery beat, it is the settling of the space Chapter 14 left completely undescribed. Establish quickly that this is old storage, not a route to anywhere external, so the chapter can move to its real engine (the search) without lingering on anticlimax. Use Aurelia's flame practically for light. No supernatural spectacle merely because a sealed door has opened.

## Scene 2 Execution — The Chest

The search must be active and physical: an initial fruitless pass, then Aurelia's spatial instinct finding the collapsed shelving, then a genuine two-person effort forcing the corroded latch. Keep this functional and earned, not decorative. Aarav's recognition of the classification mark should read as ordinary trained literacy — he has seen enough of the current system to recognize this one predates it — not as any special sense.

## Scene 3 Execution — The Proximity Beat

This is the chapter's one deliberate break from the Ch11–14 proximity pattern: Aurelia holds her flame close and steady so Aarav can read brittle paper safely. Write it as purely functional — the physical requirements of reading fragile material by firelight in an unlit space — with no lingering, no unnamed feeling, no post-beat distance-recalibration language. It should read as unremarkable to both characters. Deliver the historical parallel (era, disturbance, ward-failure notation) through their reading and reaction, not a block of exposition. End the scene on the bare, unqualified comparison — do not let Aarav's caveat leak in early.

## Scene 4 Execution — The Midpoint

This is the chapter's central engineering challenge. Follow the exact sequence locked in `scene-breakdown.md`: caveat opens the scene → further brief search → the slip is found → Aarav's self-correction → Aurelia's connective leap → Aurelia's embodied stopped-motion beat. Do not compress this into a single paragraph of realization; do not expand it into a debate. The realization must be **constructed from the evidence in front of them**, not delivered as authorial summary.

Aurelia's realization must not read as "I was right." Write it as the harder, quieter thing: her correct instinct has nothing left to point at. Aarav's self-correction must occupy its own distinct beat, in language that does not echo his Chapter 14 line ("I took the conclusion instead of the evidence underneath it") — this time the object is a document's precise wording, not a person's authority, and the sentence shape should reflect that difference.

## Scene 5 Execution — The Transcription

Keep the camera on the physical act: pen, paper, checking lines against the original, the chest, the chamber around them. This is not a conversation scene. If dialogue occurs, it should be sparse and functional (confirming a line, noting damage), never a debate about what to do next — the "what to do next" is already being enacted. End on the completed transcription and the original left in place — a physical image, not a stated plan.

## Evidence Chain (For Drafting Reference)

| Stage | Content | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Physical observation | Decommissioned storage annex; corroded record chest; a file bearing a mark consistent with the current restricted-holdings classification convention | OBSERVED/DOCUMENTED |
| Historical evidence (main fragment) | A witnessed disturbance, generations old, which the boundary wards failed to register | OBSERVED/DOCUMENTED, within the surviving fragment |
| Comparison | Shares the ward-failure trait with the Chapter 4 anomaly | INFERENCE — resemblance, explicitly not identity |
| Historical evidence (slip) | No point of ingress was established at the time | OBSERVED/DOCUMENTED, within its own stated scope |
| Midpoint realization | The external-entry assumption was never validated — historically or now | INFERENCE — the chapter's earned conclusion |
| NOT ESTABLISHED | The phenomenon's true nature; whether historical and current events are the same; who reclassified the file and why; any connection to SERPENTFIRE, the Unknown Student, the Ch11 marker, or the Ch13 marks | Must remain unknown |

The chain must culminate, in order, in:
1. The corridor/chamber is real.
2. It does not establish an external-entry route for the current phenomenon.
3. The historical record documents a materially comparable unexplained event.
4. The historical case contains no established evidence of external entry.
5. Therefore the current external-entry theory is no longer sufficient.
6. The actual phenomenon remains unexplained.
7. The new investigative direction is toward what Veilward has forgotten.

Do not collapse these steps into a single stated conclusion.

## Chapter 10 Payoff

**Chapter 10 established:** a previous, generically-described witnessed disturbance sharing only the ward-failure trait with the Ch4 anomaly; a record terminating at "sealed supplement, ref. 14-C" in restricted holdings; a restriction notation showing the file was reclassified after original filing, for unknown reasons; Voss confirmed the restriction exists but refused to explain it.

**Chapter 15 adds:** the sealed supplement's original contents — the outline of the historical disturbance and the historical investigation's inconclusive finding (no ingress established) — via the discovery that the chamber is the physical remnant of an old, decommissioned restricted-holdings annex, never fully migrated when the Archive reorganized.

**Why the connection is legitimate:** this is an authored addition consistent with, not dictated by, Chapter 10's finalized text — verified against fresh reads of Ch10's finalization record and handoff; it contradicts nothing there.

**What remains inaccessible/incomplete:** who reclassified the file and why (a separate, later, still fully unresolved act, distinct from the record's original inconclusive closure).

**What Chapter 15 deliberately does not resolve:** whether the historical and current events are the same phenomenon; the reclassification's actor and motive.

## Protected Lore

Do not reveal or explain: Outer Dark, Seeker, Harbinger, First Ember, Elyra, Varun, Lost Path, true Veil origin/weakness, true breach theory, Echo identity/origin/motive, hidden chamber, SERPENTFIRE's meaning, Unknown Student's identity, or the phenomenon's true nature/mechanism/agency.

SERPENTFIRE must not be introduced or explained in Chapter 15. Chapter 12 having introduced the word creates no pressure for this chapter to use it.

## Relationship

Keep rivalry alive underneath. Break the four-chapter proximity-avoidance pattern in Scene 3 — functional proximity is allowed and encouraged, with zero romantic charge. Scene 4's realization must be simultaneous and argument-free, the first time in the book this pair reaches a conclusion without disagreement. Scene 5's transcription is the trust-earning act: collaborative, wordless, Aurelia checking rather than directing. No friendship declaration, no romance, no emotional-intimacy escalation.

## World and Magic

Show old infrastructure, institutional layering, physical signs of age and change — through action, not description blocks. Damage source is ordinary age and neglect in a dry, sealed space — not water, fire, or violence. No new magical systems, terminology, measurements, formal experiments, unexplained spectacle, or sentient environmental behavior. Aarav's classification-mark recognition is institutional literacy, not a magical ability.

## Pacing

Required progression: **immediate consequence → physical discovery → historical evidence → constructed reversal → changed investigative direction.**

Avoid: repetitive archival searching (do not re-describe the same debris/shelving between Scenes 2 and 4), long static dialogue, cataloguing the four mystery threads in list form, excessive internal explanation, another walk-away ending, another "we don't have a clean answer" formulation, another Chapter 14-style externally-triggered epistemic correction, generic ominous atmosphere used instead of evidence.

## Prose Watchlist

Carried forward from prior chapter audits and specific to this chapter's risks:
- overuse of "specific,"
- repeated "the way [subject][verb]" constructions,
- mirrored/triple negation,
- trailing "which" clauses,
- clustering of "already,"
- lightly-hedged external-attribution technique (Ch13–14 pattern) applied to Aarav's demeanor,
- "didn't have a clean answer" and structural equivalents,
- cataloguing the four mystery threads in list form (Ch13's "three things that don't fit anywhere" was a one-time device — do not repeat it verbatim or templated),
- walk-away endings,
- proximity-avoidance/unnamed-feeling constructions in Scene 3,
- echoing Aarav's Chapter 14 self-correction phrasing/rhythm in Scene 4.

Do not turn this into mechanical prose. The goal is natural variation, not word-count policing.

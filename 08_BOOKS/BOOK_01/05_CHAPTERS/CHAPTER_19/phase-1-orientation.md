## 5. Elyra / Varun Inscription Function

**Establishes**
- Two named individuals existed.
- They were recorded deliberately and formally in carved stone, so they were once worth the effort.
- The record sits *inside the Academy's own fabric*.
- Names are legible enough to read, and the surrounding text is damaged.
- One fragment shows conflict and a fire.

**Suggests, without asserting**
- Institutional memory has a gap: named once, not carried forward.
- The damage is uneven, but its cause is left undetermined (weathering, hand or intervention).
- The trouble predates anything they have handled, undated.
- The fragment leans one way, toward the pair as opposed parties. Ch25's "The fire was never meant to belong to either of them" must be able to reframe that lean.

**Must NOT reveal**
- Everything in the prompt's protected list.
- The relationship type. Audit Clue 9 says "their names must not imply a relationship or reveal their final fate in Book One," which is stricter than the prompt's lock.
- Species, era or fate.
- The First Ember by name.
- Any wording that pre-empts Ch25's "belong / either of them."
- Any equation of "ancient fire" with SERPENTFIRE.
- Any Echo link.

**Why now:** Eighteen chapters have asked *what happened*, and Act II-B's question is "What has Veilward forgotten?" The mystery now needs a *who*. Clue 9 is scheduled here, and Ch25 needs a fair-play seed six chapters earlier.

**Chapter question (what the reader is left asking):** *Who were the two people Veilward's own memory can't account for, and what did they know that mattered enough for that to be true?*

- **Identity:** who they were.
- **Institutional memory:** "can't account for."
- **Inscription:** the physical fact.
- **Purpose:** the Academy exists to remember.
- **Aurelia and Aarav's species:** deliberately excluded. Any mirror effect is the reader's inference, never on-page.
- **Echo:** deliberately excluded. The reader may connect it, but the chapter may not.

## 6. World-Orientation Opportunity

**The one piece: what Veilward is for.** It exists so that responsibility for others passes across generations, and it keeps a *remembrance* of those who carried it. That answers "what does the Academy prepare students for" in emotional terms without a lecture.

**Mechanism:** the *context* of the inscription, not its text. The names sit within a surface the Academy still maintains, so the reader sees what the Academy remembers and what two damaged names look like against it. Aurelia can feel the contrast between what the Academy *measures* (the rankings board) and what it *remembers*, which resonates with her Ch17 arithmetic.

**Canon support:** [veilward-academy.md](03_ACADEMY/veilward-academy.md) covers Protection (§2), Duty (§16), Remembrance (§46: "names may have been forgotten, misrepresented, deliberately removed") and honor (§45).

**Fallback mechanism, if remembrance is rejected in Phase 2.1:** an ordinary evaluation task built on the Duty pillar, which sends Aurelia to the material for a non-mystery reason.

**Limits**
- Do not define the Veil.
- Do not name Outer Dark.
- Do not explain Phoenix/Naga, rankings-to-future or the wards. These stay unassigned book-level orientation debts, and I recommend the author assign owners.
- The "Remembrance" surface is open canon ("named traditions remain open"), so Phase 2.1 must integrate it.

**Discouraged and blocked source mechanisms**

## 7. Aurelia's Investigative Agency

She must not be an "I found it, Aarav interprets" node.

1. **Route.** She reaches the inscriptions by a path Aarav isn't on (the evaluation-driven candidate above), not through his research.
2. **Recognition.** She was first to notice the Mark in Ch5. Recognizing it across contexts is a visual and instinctive contribution he wouldn't have made.
3. **Observation.** She uses Phoenix-flavored *physical* literacy: what fire does to stone and what it doesn't. This is a mundane observation, not fire-on-stone and not reading history from a place (which the magic lock forbids). It can *rule out* a cause. For example, the fragment says "fire" but the damage isn't heat, which contradicts the obvious assumption without settling anything.
4. **Challenge.** She contests Aarav's reading of the fragment or his caution, grounded in observation rather than mood.
5. **Choice.** She sets the terms of what goes to Voss and how. The cost of that choice is hers.

**Rule:** the first *decisive* interpretive frame is hers. Aarav may revise it but not originate it. Her voice should be quicker and more instinctive than Ch18's account, which the review noted drifted toward his diction.

## 9. Tension / Friction Engine

**Primary pressure: the reporting asymmetry.** Ch18's event was thin evidence. The inscription is physical, survives any question, and is squarely an *inquiry* under Voss's rule. So they must tell him *that day*, but telling him invites the very questions ("what led you there, anything else?") that put the Ch18 silence under test. Their own wording ("not when it suits us") comes due, and the cost lands on Aurelia: her evaluation window, her file, her stated principle. It is a *partial-disclosure* pressure, new to the book and distinct from Ch18's silence and Ch16's door.

**Secondary: an interpretive disagreement** between them. It stays *proxy-level*, arguing the stone while the real issues go unnamed. Ch20 owns the honest, fear-level argument, so Ch19 must not pre-empt it.

**Why it differs from the equanimity pattern:** the pressure comes from their own commitments, cannot be resolved by more checking, and leaves a residue instead of a concession. That satisfies the review's "cost of being wrong lands on one of them." The design assumes the review's Query about Aarav's disclosure drift is *intended and now noticed*, the only in-manuscript remedy while Ch18 is frozen.

## 10. Mystery Convergence Decision

**Choice: one-thread convergence (Option B), with the Angular Mark.**

**Why the Mark**
- It is entitled by schedule: the recurring-symbol Appearance Three and the audit's Ch12–13 repeat.
- Clue map #9 ("symbol in restricted history") sits immediately before #10 (Elyra/Varun fragments).
- The reader has forgotten it and the review calls it the strongest early hook.
- It matches the medium (cut marks in stone).
- It is Aurelia's own clue (she saw it first).

**Guardrails**
- Its relationship to the names stays *unanswered*. It is present in the same setting but not inscribed as their emblem, and separated by surface, scale or depth.
- One character speculation at most, raised and parked.
- "The symbol tells the reader the mystery has a history. It does not reveal what that history is."

**Why not A:** the symbol repeat is a scheduled debt, and the reader is at tolerance for unlinked threads.

**Why not C:** capacity and canon.
- Statement-matching would breach the Echo-link hazard.
- Serpentfire may be acknowledged but not defined.
- Three or more threads would make the "thread inventory" chapter.

**Review vs reader-sim disagreements, resolved by canon**
- **Serpentfire.** Review: "healthy." Sim: "deferred too long." The audit says Book 1 "must not define Serpentfire," and repeated references are planned. So the sim's impatience is answered by a brief hedged acknowledgement, not an advance.
- **Convergence.** The sim wants at least two threads linked. The review wants each thread's fate decided. The outline and audit authorize exactly one link (the symbol repeat).
- **Mira.** The sim wants more in the *middle* chapters. The review says the cast is complete. Ch19 is not a middle chapter, so Mira is not forced (see §15).

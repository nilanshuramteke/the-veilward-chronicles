### 5. Five-Scene Execution Map

#### SCENE 1 — THE WRONG KIND OF DAMAGE (900–1,100)

**Objective:** Aurelia independently discovers the damaged inscription, makes the physical observations, recognizes the Mark, forms Neglect, and decides to have her reading tested.

**Entry state:**
- Morning after Ch18, nine days from evaluation.
- She is in motion on a practical errand for an unwatched prep hour. The errand is not a drill, a board, a classroom or a training exercise.
- She has chosen a route that avoids the east passage, for a bookkeeping reason and not a fear: if it happened while she was alone, she would owe Voss "that day." This is Ch18's rule honored, in one or two lines and with no restaging.
- She is carrying something to write on. The prep isn't working.

**Beats (proportions, not quotas):**
1. **Ordinary movement, errand, route choice** (~20%). Open on an object or an action, with no atmosphere and no mystery.
2. **The stretch of wall registers as wrong** (~15%). She has never come this way. The space is unnamed: no roll, custodian, ceremony, columns or hall name. Other carved names are visibly of different ages and are texture only, unquoted, uncatalogued and unread.
3. **Surface loss, provisionally not heat-type** (~10%). This is her first read, and it is visual only.
4. **ELYRA, then VARUN** (~10%). Both are legible, on separate lines, and hit as unexpected. No explanation, no reaction narrating importance, and the names are not read aloud.
5. **The surrounding text is gone** (~10%). This is the "damaged context" moment: the loss becomes meaningful relative to the names. No legible connective.
6. **The fragment** (~12%). It contains a strife-class word ("strife" recommended), "fire," and an age-word ("old" recommended). "Old fire" may be the only adjacent legible pair. No verbs, no pronouns, no subject or object.
7. **Second, deliberate heat check** (~8%). The word "fire" invites the obvious reading, so she re-confirms in one or two lines from her own experience with scorched material (her own fire, Ch1; scorched targets, Ch3; scorched sheets, Ch12). She rules one thing out and explains nothing.
8. **The Mark** (~8%). It is near the stretch and shallow, hand-cut. Describe it only as three straight strokes that don't quite close, with a gap. Use "shape" or "mark" (her Ch6 word is "shape"). She recognizes it from the post and the rail. Say nothing of reporting history.
9. **Neglect** (~7%). It rests on use: she has never come this way, and everyone walks past unread. One optional clause of interior contrast (rankings measure, this keeps) may appear, and it is cut if it reads abstract.
10. **Copy and decision** (~5%). She makes a passive hand transcription of the visible text and a rough sketch of the Mark on her own paper. She knows this is at the edge of Ch16 (one clause). She decides she wants the reading tested and will bring Aarav. The scene cuts there, with no delivery mechanics, runner or lateness.

**Information:** the names, the loss, the fragment, the Mark's return in stone, and Neglect. Nothing else.

**Dialogue intent:** none. Zero spoken lines.

**Physical staging:**
- Her movements are walking, slowing, stopping, crouching or tilting for a low line, and changing angle for natural light.
- She keeps a working distance. No hand touches stone.
- No flame, glow or light-conjuring.
- The copy is done on paper against her own knee or arm.

**Exit state:** She has a working reading, copies, and a decision to test it.

**Forbidden drift:**
- Archaeology-report cataloguing and measurements.
- Atmosphere ("silent," "still," "old-feeling").
- Dread, chills or supernatural sensation.
- Agentive damage words (erased, chiseled, scraped, hacked, smoothed, removed, covered up).
- Funerary or date wording.
- Naming the space.
- Explaining the Mark.
- Serpentfire association.
- Reporting-status thoughts about the Mark.
- More than one functional mention of Aarav (the closing decision).

#### SCENE 2 — TWO READINGS (1,200–1,500)

**Objective:** Contested reading, and Aurelia's decision to report.

**Entry state:**
- Same wall, with the bell approaching.
- Aarav is present at her request. Establish it in a clause: no arrival drama, no runner, and he is not late.
- She is already re-checking something on the wall.

**Beats (fast, tactical, time-boxed):**
1. She states Neglect, resting on use and not forensics.
2. **Aarav names what the evidence can't establish** and states Choice as inability to exclude: the absence of context may not be accidental. His anchor is the Ch16 "closed and explained" exchange. Alluding to it, not quoting it, works well because the line was Aurelia's own.
3. **She names what would change her mind.** It is a social test (ask anyone who has been here longer), not a new physical finding.
4. **Aarav offers an alternative fitting the same evidence:** the same answer would look identical from both readings.
5. He asks how sure she is about heat. She re-checks and holds. **He accepts it on her experience** (his concession).
6. The Mark's shape is confirmed against his shed mark (at most "the same kind" from him). She parks one question, whether the Mark and the names are related. He refuses it as unanswerable and says he can't date the Mark against the wall. It is dropped, with no hypothesis, no authorship claim and no recent-cutter claim.
7. **She accepts the uncertainty** (her concession) and refuses to delay. Her reasons: she recognized the Mark, which makes this material and not a curiosity; word reaches Voss within the hour; and her own standard.
8. **"Record first."** Aarav wants the transcription cross-checked and the Mark held back until then. She does not yield, and the beat is time-boxed by the bell.
9. He cross-reads the transcription against her copy, at working distance. She doesn't wait for him to finish.
10. **One beat:** she registers that he has decided not to say something. She does not know what and does not decode it.
11. She leaves for Voss and he stays to finish.

**Information:** both frames, agreement on the physical facts, the Mark confirmed by shape, the parked question, and that neither knows who the names were. This seeds her readiness to answer "do you know them?"

**Dialogue intent:**
- Each line is anchored to something visible ("that line," "here").
- No abstractions.
- The structure is claim, limit, falsifier, alternative, acceptance.
- Aarav speaks in two sentences or fewer, conditionally, and never states a conclusion.
- Neither uses blame, guilt, "falsely accused," "erased" or "covered up."
- Aarav never mentions or negotiates the earlier sightings or the omission.

**Physical staging:**
- They stand at **opposite ends** of the wall.
- No shared lamp, no flanking of the two names, and no shoulder proximity.
- No touching or measuring.
- Aarav writes by hand.

**Exit state:** She goes to Voss. Aarav stays. The frames are unresolved.

**Forbidden drift:**
- Ch13 replay (she yields, or the stairwell argument).
- Aarav as chapter analyst or intellectual winner.
- A long philosophical debate.
- Any Mark hypothesis.
- Ch18 lines or the Ch18 rule invoked as a reason to report.
- Aarav's Ch8 comparison.
- The Fourteen-C copies.
- Choice sliding to cover-up.

#### SCENE 3 — BEFORE HE HEARS IT (500–700)

**Objective:** Aurelia's disclosure at a cost. The tell begins.

**Entry state:** Mid-to-late morning. She finds Voss where he is working. It is not his office (Ch17), not a corridor intercept (Ch16), and not the Ch14 records room. No new named locations. He is on his feet mid-task, and she does not knock-and-wait.

**Beats:**
1. She is ready for the natural question about the names, with her answer prepared. This is one clause, and it is not narrated as noticing.
2. She reports the wall in order: location, names, damaged inscription, fragment. It is evidence only, and she offers neither frame.
3. She reports the Mark at the wall, then **volunteers her own sightings only: the rail (during the event) and the post (the next day)**. She states plainly, in her own plain diction, that she should have reported this earlier. One clause may recall Ch14's "everything" in paraphrase. It is the chapter's only callback. She says Aarav found another, and that it is his to describe.
4. **Voss states the omission flatly.** It was left out of her statement, and again after his "Everything." He gives no adjectives, no lecture, no sanction, no consequence announced, and no praise.
5. **His questions are operational only:** where exactly, who else has seen it, whether anything was touched. Include at least one that sits physically beside the names (for example, how much of the surrounding text is legible). None concern the names.
6. He goes to the wall. The scene ends on his action or direction.

**Information:** the wall, the Mark's history, the earlier omission, and that Aarav has a sighting.

**Dialogue intent:**
- She speaks in a straight sequence.
- Voss has about five short utterances, all questions or directions.
- He doesn't explain the procedure yet.

**Physical staging:** Voss keeps working while she reports, with no reaction beats. He shows no reaction to the Mark, the names or the confession.

**Exit state:** The Mark has entered institutional knowledge, and she has paid for it. Voss is heading to the wall.

**Forbidden drift:**

- Voss explaining, praising, punishing, threatening or acknowledging the names.
- A new restriction.
- Ch17 echoes: a knock, a seated Voss, "All right," her statement made before he can ask, "the conversation was over."
- Aurelia over-apologizing or narrating her arc.
- A silence tag after her admission.
- The Echo raised.

#### SCENE 4 — AT THE WALL (1,300–1,600)

**Objective:** Voss examines the evidence. His knowledge shows only through omission. The record architecture is set, and the scene ends on his instruction.

**Entry state:** Late morning, at the wall. Aarav has finished the transcription and is present. He learns from Voss's direct question that she named his sighting. He didn't know she would disclose the Mark history.

**Beats:**
1. **Voss reads the wall.** He checks Aarav's transcription line by line against the stone, then the fragment and the Mark, and passes over the names as ordinary line entries without a question. Render this as the order of his questions and actions, never as gaze.
2. **What was touched and what was copied.** She answers narrowly: text and a rough sketch of the Mark. He may note in one flat sentence that copying is at the edge of Ch16, as a minor cost, with no sanction. Neither of them connects the word "copied" to the Ch15 copies.
3. **The route question.** He asks what brought her here. She gives the true prep reason and leaves out the east-passage reason. Aarav is silent because he wasn't asked.
4. **Voss asks Aarav directly** whether he has seen the shape before (the shed mark). Aarav confirms plainly and does not overrule her. Any invitation to comment on her sequence gets an answer about the evidence only.
5. **"What do you make of it?"** Asked once, generically. Each answers in one sentence with no debate, and Voss doesn't comment.
6. **Her observation.** One declarative sentence: he didn't ask who they were. No "why," and no follow-up.
7. **His answer is Level 1 plus procedural reticence.** He will say what he thinks after reading what each of them saw and knew, because anything he says now ends up in their accounts. He doesn't confirm or deny prior knowledge, doesn't lie, and gives no history. It is a single sentence.
8. **The orders,** in a few precise instructions and not a lecture:
   - (a) A joint record of the wall's physical facts, under both names.
   - (b) An individual written statement from each, by end of day and not compared, of what they knew about the Mark and this wall, and when, including anything not brought to him. **Oldest first.**
   - (c) He will examine the wall himself. He does not restrict or close it off.
   - The distinction (D8): the wall is a finding, and what each of them knew is not.
9. **The scene ends on the "oldest first" instruction.** The order lands as an obligation.

**Information:** the tell, the joint-record and statement instruments, and the scoped instruction.

**Dialogue intent:**
- Voss carries the scene through short procedural sentences and asks the questions in the order above.
- Aurelia has about five lines and Aarav three or four.
- Voss offers no lore and doesn't link the names, the Mark, the Echo or Ember.
- Do not reuse Ch12's "isn't cleared," and do not have him take or lock anything.

**Physical staging:**
- Voss works close to the wall.
- Aurelia and Aarav stand back at working distance, apart from each other and not flanking anything.
- No exchanged glances and no post-scene conference.

**Exit state:** They are sent to write, apart. Aurelia knows he didn't ask, and that Aarav is carrying something she can't name.

**Forbidden drift:**
- A second tell (any pause, stillness, facial or voice change, hand movement, looking away or dramatic recognition).
- An interrogation.
- Voss explaining or reassuring.
- Aurelia escalating.
- A replay of the frames debate.
- A "they'd have to wait" ending, a silence or a door.
- Any hint of Level 2.
- The east passage in the instruction.

- She thinks in fast, physical, material, decisive terms.
- Her verbs are direct: looks, crouches, tilts, compares, decides.
- Her sentences run short and declarative, with one longer run when she is sure.
- Self-correction is a turn in her thinking, not a list, and appears at most twice in the chapter.
- Her controlled irritation is the dominant emotional texture.
- Her heat-damage knowledge is expressed in body-and-hands vocabulary.
- She never reasons in institutional terms ("protocol," "documentation," "chain").
- She prefers "shape."
- She is plain under pressure.
- She is willing to test why she might be wrong.

**Aarav:**
- He is precise, conditional and economical.
- He gives two sentences or fewer.
- He separates what the evidence supports from what it doesn't.
- He never states a conclusion or uses verbs of intent (hid, removed, covered).
- He is not the chapter's winner.

**Voss:**
- His sentences are short and complete.
- His questions are procedural and ordered.
- He gives instructions about the record and never asks about feelings.
- He uses no adjectives, reassurance or explanation.
- He uses only baseline Ch9/14/16 cues, each once at most (folded hands is baseline, not the tell).

# Chapter 19 — Phase 2 Detailed Scene Plan

Planning only. No prose, no repository changes.

## Planning Basis (source findings that shaped the plan)

These came from re-reading the repository. Where they differ from Phase 1 assumptions, they govern.

1. **Voss already knows the names.** [author-hidden-truths.md](00_CORE/author-hidden-truths.md) (Voss section) says he found contradictory records suggesting Elyra and Varun "were not responsible for the disaster in the way history claimed," and chose silence. His on-page behavior in Ch19 must be consistent with prior knowledge. Nothing about *what* he knows may show.
2. **The Mark was never reported to anyone.**
   - I searched Ch5–18 and found no report to Voss, Seraphine or any instructor.
   - It was absent from the Ch14 formal disclosure and from the Ch13 list of "three things that don't fit."
   - Phase 1 called reporting the inscription a possible exposure of "previous silence." The Mark is that silence in concrete form, and it is not the Echo event. This gives the disclosure pressure a real, non-Echo cost.
3. **Voss's standing rules.**
   - Ch9: findings come out as "One record. Not his version and then, separately, yours."
   - Ch16: "Anything further happens through me, directly, every time," and "I heard within the hour" of a classroom remark. News reaches him fast, which supplies a clock.
4. **The Ch18 rule's actual wording.** "If it happens again, or if anything survives a question, he hears it that day" refers to the *Echo event*. The inscription is not a trigger for that rule. It is an inquiry under Voss's Ch16 terms. Disclosing Ch18 is therefore Aurelia's *choice*, not an obligation, until something forces it.
5. **Geography.** Ch16 puts the practicum gallery near the east stair, and Ch18's east passage runs from that stair. The memorial site must be outside that zone or it implies an Echo link.
6. **Devices already used, to avoid:**
   - maintenance crews exposing old fabric (Ch13, 14, 17)
   - a runner summons (Ch16) and a Voss corridor intercept (Ch16)
   - Voss's office (Ch10, 17)
   - lamp-lit shoulder proximity (Ch13, 15)
   - a late arrival (Ch18)
   - Ch17's walk-the-line-and-compare-wear stone scene
7. **Ch25 and audit protections.** Ch25's line "The fire was never meant to belong to either of them" is reserved. Audit Clue 9: names "must not imply a relationship or reveal their final fate."
8. **Timeline.** Ch17 was eleven days from the evaluation, and Ch18 said ten. If Ch19 is the next morning, "nine" is arithmetically safe. Use a number only if Phase 2.1 confirms. Avoid seasonal words and "weeks."

## Scene 1 — The Wrong Kind of Damage

**Location:** The memorial passage, in a wing other than the east-stair/east-passage/kitchen-yard zone. Phase 2.1 fixes the exact site from existing campus canon.
**Time:** The morning after Ch18. Early and mostly empty.
**POV:** Aurelia

**Primary Function:** Discovery, physical observation, Mark recognition and her first interpretive frame, all solo. It also establishes what the place is *for* by context.

**Aurelia's Objective:** Get an hour of real evaluation prep done unwatched. The evaluation is now single-digit days away and staying on the index work has already cost her the hours.

**Immediate Obstacle:** She is taking a long route to avoid the east passage.
- Her reason is *rule-based*, not fear. If it happened again with only her account to offer, she would owe Voss "that day."
- She is annoyed at herself for rerouting.
- The detour is what puts her in an unfamiliar passage.
- Her mind won't stay on the folder.

**Aarav's Objective:** Not present.

**Conflict / Friction:** Internal and physical. The prep isn't working, and one stretch of the wall is *wrong* in a way she can't dismiss.

**Discovery / Evidence** (escalating in this order):
1. The surface loss on the panel is not heat damage. She knows what heat does to stone (colour change, crazing, flaking), and this has none of it. That contradicts the assumption that a text mentioning fire would be fire-damaged.
2. The panel is separate from the ordered columns. Inside it, two names are legible on separate lines, and the surrounding text is largely gone.
3. The names are cut deeper than the text around them. Both deep cutting and later re-cutting are possible, and she can't tell which.
4. The Mark, low at the panel's edge. She recognizes it by the gap. She was first to notice it (Ch5) and can picture the post.
5. The fragment. A word for dispute, "fire," and an age-word, all broken.

**What the Reader Learns:**
- Names, and a separate damaged panel with a fragment about dispute and an old fire.
- The Mark exists again, in *stone*, in Academy fabric.
- Veilward keeps a wall of names that students walk past unread. The roll's lowest rows are recent, so the keeping continues.
- One physical line contrasts what the Academy *measures* (a rankings sheet) with what it *keeps*.

**What Remains Unknown:** What the names mean, who cut them, why they're separate, what the fragment refers to, and the Mark's relationship to any of it.

**Aurelia's Agency:**
- Her own route, reached by a choice of hers.
- Her own physical reasoning (the not-heat finding and the depth asymmetry).
- Her own recognition of the Mark.
- Her first frame is *Neglect*, formed before anyone else sees it.
- She records the panel herself (a careful hand copy of names, fragment and Mark) and touches nothing else.
- She does not "call Aarav." She decides she wants her reading challenged before she takes it anywhere. This is the "rather be wrong out loud" habit made deliberate.

**Relationship Movement:** Respect only. One interior beat that she wants *his* check because he's the person who will find the hole. No attraction language.

**Mystery State Change:**
- Angular Mark: dormant → *found in older material*.
- Elyra and Varun: introduced.
- Serpentfire: the word "fire" prompts a *parked* thought (see Scene 2).

**Disclosure Pressure:** Seeded. She knows Voss hears fast and that an unwatched hour in a public passage won't stay unknown. She already suspects she'll be asked why she was here.

**Transition:** She has a frame, a copy and a deadline. Passage traffic will begin at the bell, and she wants the reading tested before she does anything else.

**Guardrails:**
- Not Ch17's walk-and-compare-wear sequence. Wear appears only as the *negative* (not heat) and the depth asymmetry.
- No fire used on stone.
- No species vocabulary near the names.
- No "fourth."
- No reference to the fourth stone or the annex groove.
- The warm-hand memory is *not* invoked.

**Word Allocation:** 900–1,100

## Scene 2 — Two Readings

**Location:** The same wall.
**Time:** Shortly after. The bell is coming and the passage will fill.
**POV:** Aurelia

**Primary Function:** The contested reading, carried by *action*, not conversation. The frames drive the tactics, and the tactics have costs.

**Aurelia's Objective:** Have her frame tested and get to Voss before word does.

**Immediate Obstacle:** A short window, and Aarav's competing read.

**Aarav's Objective:** Understand it and *record it before anything changes* (the Ch15 instinct and the annex/corridor precedent). He does not dispute that Voss hears it today.

**Conflict / Friction:**
- **Interpretive.** She states *Neglect* as a claim *with a prediction*, meaning what would prove her wrong. He offers *Choice*, built on the same evidence: names sturdier than the reason, and "dispute" in the fragment.
- **Tactical.** Her frame says the institution doesn't know, so tell it now. His says it may know and may not want this found, so record first and don't lead with the Mark, because it exposes a silence that dates to the day after the breach.
- Both are principled. Neither is proven right.
- Staging: they work at opposite ends of the panel, at different heights. No shared-lamp proximity and no symmetrical flanking of the two names.

**Discovery / Evidence** (partial, split):
- Aarav accepts the not-heat finding *on her expertise*. That is the trust beat.
- He runs one check of his own, on how the panel meets the columns. It is *ambiguous*.
- The Mark's shape matches the shed mark he found (Ch5) and is too shallow to date against the damage.
- **One restrained Mark hypothesis** (hers): the Mark may be *older than the incident*, so the field wasn't where it started. He hedges that the stone could be older than the mark. Time only, never authorship. It is parked and not repeated.
- **Serpentfire:** she notices "fire" echoes the page Voss took. She declines to lean on it. It rests on one word, and her Ch5 discipline applies ("I believe exactly what I can currently prove"). One exchange at most, with no definition and no equation. She does not think of the warm hand.

**What the Reader Learns:**
- The interpretive fork: forgotten by neglect, or by choice.
- Both leads agree on the physical facts and disagree on what they mean.
- The Mark is confirmed as the *same shape* in an older setting, and the reader is prompted to remember it.

**What Remains Unknown:** Which frame is right, the Mark's relationship to the names, and the damage cause.

**Aurelia's Agency:**
- Her frame leads and her predictions are testable.
- She challenges his caution *with a reason*: word reaches Voss within the hour, and better he hears it from them.
- She sets the priority: report today.
- The compromise is hers. She gives Aarav the interval to finish his record and takes the reporting herself.

**Relationship Movement:**
- Respect under disagreement.
- Aarav trusts her physical read.
- She grants his ambiguity finding.
- They do not reconcile the frames.
- She notices, at a pause, that he has decided not to say something. She has no idea what, and it may or may not be about the wall.

**Mystery State Change:** Mark and inscription linked *by proximity only*. Both frames stay alive.

**Disclosure Pressure:** Explicit for the first time. The question is *who and what*, not whether. What goes in, and does the Mark go in? Aarav says "not yet." She says it must.

**Transition:** She decides. He stays to record and keep the wall undisturbed. She leaves for Voss.

**Guardrails:**
- No "you broke your rule" line.
- No romantic or mirror staging.
- Nothing about what the fire "belonged" to.
- No statement that either frame is confirmed.

**Word Allocation:** 1,200–1,500

## Scene 3 — Before He Hears It

**Location:** Wherever Voss is at that hour. Not his office (Ch17) and not a corridor intercept (Ch16). *She* goes to *him*.
**Time:** Mid-morning, and quick.
**POV:** Aurelia

**Primary Function:** Her independent choice, made at personal cost. It is deliberately short and fast, in contrast to the earlier Voss scenes.

**Aurelia's Objective:** Get Voss to *come and see it* rather than merely be told, and say what she must before he hears it elsewhere.

**Immediate Obstacle:** She cannot say what the Mark is without stating how long she has held it.

**Aarav's Objective:** Absent. He is at the wall.

**Conflict / Friction:**
- Aurelia against herself: the sequence of honesty.
- Voss's first move is *operational*. He asks where, who has seen it, whether anything was touched, and whether Aarav is with it. He does **not** ask who the names are (the first tell).
- She has rehearsed that question. She knows it is the natural one, has nothing to answer it with, and it never comes.

**Discovery / Evidence:**
- Voss learns of the inscription, the names and the fragment.
- He learns of the Mark and that it was cut in the training-field post, the shed and the rail.
- He learns *when* she first saw it: the day after the breach.

**What the Reader Learns:**
- Aurelia volunteers a cost, and it is the oldest silence, not the newest one.
- Voss's reaction is a cold, procedural gravity about the silence, and then he says "show me."
- The reader can register what he did *not* ask.

**What Remains Unknown:** What Voss knows.

**Aurelia's Agency:** She decides the order and the terms: physical facts, the Mark, and a request that he see the wall. She takes the consequence herself and does not hide behind the joint account.

**Relationship Movement:** Small friction. She has gone past Aarav's "not yet." Trust means she did not undercut his recording window.

**Mystery State Change:** The Mark enters institutional knowledge (Disclosure state: ADVANCE).

**Disclosure Pressure:** Acute. She has chosen the honest-and-costly route on the old silence. The Ch18 event is not raised, and she knows what that means.

**Transition:** Voss goes to the wall himself, with her.

**Guardrails:**
- No runner, no office and no long interrogation.
- Voss does not explain, threaten or stonewall.
- No door closes.

**Word Allocation:** 500–700

## Scene 4 — At the Wall

**Location:** The memorial passage, at the panel.
**Time:** Late morning. The traffic pause after the bell gives a window.
**POV:** Aurelia

**Primary Function:** The institutional response. It produces the chapter's contradiction and its immediate consequence.

**Aurelia's Objective:** Have the physical evidence read by someone with authority, and see how he reads it.

**Immediate Obstacle:** Voss's controlled surface, and the presence of Aarav, who has not agreed to everything she has already said.

**Aarav's Objective:** Deliver the physical record cleanly and *not* overrule her in front of Voss.

**Conflict / Friction:**
- **The tell.** Voss's eyes go to the fragment and the Mark *first*, not to the names. Someone who already knew the names would not need to read them.
- The route question ("What brought you here?") has two true answers. One is evaluation prep. The other is that she was avoiding the east passage. She gives the first, and the reader knows it is partial. Aarav stays silent. He does not add or correct, which respects "it's your account." She notes it is not the same silence as hers.
- Asked what they make of it, they give *different* frames.
- **Aurelia's direct question**, in the "wrong out loud" register of Ch18: he didn't ask who the names were.

**Discovery / Evidence:**
- Aarav's record.
- Aurelia's not-heat and depth-asymmetry points, offered as physical observations.
- Voss's minimal verbal acknowledgment: he has encountered both names before, cannot account for them, does not know what they mean, and will not speculate.
- His reason is *contamination of their accounts*. He wants to know what each saw before they talk themselves into one story.

**What the Reader Learns:**
- Someone at the Academy has known these names and said nothing. That contradicts "the Academy forgot," which both leads were working from.
- Voss cannot explain them.
- Their divergent frames now have an institutional consequence.

**What Remains Unknown:**
- Where or how Voss knows the names.
- Species, relationship, era, fate and cause.
- Why he kept silent. The reader may suspect, and nothing is confirmed.

**Aurelia's Agency:**
- She reads the evidence live in front of authority, physically.
- She notices the tell and says it aloud instead of filing it.
- She leads with the Mark.
- She makes the route choice knowingly.

**Relationship Movement:**
- Aarav backs her *evidence* and does not endorse her *sequence* (a single, plain acknowledgment).
- Neither smooths it over.
- Respect holds without reconciliation.

**Mystery State Change:** Institutional secrecy advances (Voss's prior knowledge is signalled). The Mark is now on the record.

**Disclosure Pressure:** Peak. Voss's order: **separate, complete written accounts today, "from the day after the breach," no comparing.** This reverses his Ch9 "one record" principle and removes the option of a partial account. "From the day after the breach" necessarily sweeps in everything they have held back.

**Transition:** They are sent to write, apart. Voss stays at the wall.

**Guardrails:**
- He does not seal or restrict the wall.
- No "you'll have to wait."
- No lore from Voss.
- He does not link the names to the Echo, the Ember or Serpentfire.
- No exposition of *why* he knows.

**Word Allocation:** 1,300–1,600

## Scene 5 — Oldest First

**Location:** Wherever she can work alone. Not a lit courtyard and not an "after-the-walk" setting.
**Time:** The same afternoon.
**POV:** Aurelia

**Primary Function:** The consequence, executed as a concrete act. It closes on a decision, not a question or an atmosphere.

**Aurelia's Objective:** Write an account she can stand behind.

**Immediate Obstacle:**
- Voss's scope covers everything.
- She and Aarav cannot compare.
- Every hour is an hour of prep she doesn't have.

**Conflict / Friction:**
- What she has to write: the Mark first, then everything after.
- The east passage is now a line she must put in or knowingly leave out of a formal, dated document.
- Her own "not when it suits us" is on the page.

**Discovery / Evidence:** None new.

**What the Reader Learns:**
- Voss's order forced her hand.
- She chooses to include the Ch18 event, marked unverified. It is her decision, made in Ch18's own terms, and Voss's scope has turned the earlier partial answer into an omission she won't sign.

**Aurelia's Agency:** She chooses. She writes chronologically, starting with the oldest silence, and puts the east passage in as the record requires.

**Relationship Movement:** Trust by *not asking*. She does not go find Aarav or look at his notes, and he does not seek hers.

**Mystery State Change:** Disclosure: ADVANCE (the Echo event is on Aurelia's record, and Aarav's account is still to come).

**Disclosure Pressure:** Aarav's own account now has to be consistent, or not, with hers, with no coordination.

**Transition:** Chapter end.

**Ending mechanics:** The last beat is her putting the east passage into the account. It is not a reflection. It is the act, and the account continues past it.

**Word Allocation:** 350–550

**Total: 4,250–5,450** (see F).

## D. Reader-Simulation Check

## F. Word-Count / Scale Recommendation

**Estimated total: 4,250–5,450 words**, with 4,500–5,000 the likely landing.

**Why sufficient.** Five scenes with five different functions carry the chapter's load: discovery, contested reading, decision, institutional response, act. Ch18 carried a comparable load in 4,434.

**Compressible.** S3 and S5 are short by design. If the draft runs long, S3 can fold into S4's opening and S5 into S4's close, giving a floor of about 3,600 words.

**More scenes or longer scenes.** If the author later raises the length target, add a scene (an ordinary-life beat, for example), not more words per scene. That is not recommended for Ch19.

**Not inflated** to meet the 100–115k book target. The story sets length.

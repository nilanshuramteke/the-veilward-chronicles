## 1. Drafting Calibration Summary

**Core objective.** Aurelia finds, reads, defends, tests, reports and records. Every consequential move in the chapter is hers: she stops, rules out heat, offers a reading, holds the decision to report, volunteers what she withheld, states one plain observation, and puts more on the record than she was asked to. The chapter should read as one person choosing to be accountable. It should not read as "clue → talk → tell authority → new rule."

**Aurelia voice target.** She thinks in fast, physical, material terms.
- Verbs are concrete: looks, crouches, tilts, compares, decides.
- Sentence rhythm is mostly short and declarative, with one longer run when she is sure of herself.
- Self-correction is a turn in her thinking, not a list: she commits, hits a snag, and adjusts. It should happen no more than twice in the chapter.
- Her irritation at lost time is the dominant texture. Fear is not.
- Her heat-damage knowledge comes from handling scorched material (Ch12), in her own body-and-hands vocabulary.
- She never reasons in institutional or procedural terms. No "records," "protocol," "documentation," or "chain."

**Aarav voice target.** He is precise, conditional and economical.
- Qualifiers: "I can't tell from this," "that would look the same if…".
- He states what the evidence does and doesn't support, one point at a time, in two sentences or fewer.
- Longer speech occurs only when he reads text aloud for the transcript.
- He never concludes. He never uses verbs of intent (hid, removed, covered, wiped).
- He is not the chapter's analyst. He gets at most two substantive claims in the whole chapter: the Choice possibility, and "record first."

**Voss performance target.** He is a complete, controlled professional with a small vocabulary.
- His sentences are instructions and procedural questions: where, when, who else has seen it, what has been touched, what has been copied.
- He gives no adjectives, no reassurance and no interpretation. He asks about no one's feelings.
- He is not menacing, evasive-for-effect, or warm.
- He carries one behavioral tell (§4), and it should be the only new thing about him.

**Relationship target.** Stage 2 / edge of Stage 3. The movement is "we disagree and it doesn't cost us." Each concedes one real thing to the other, and neither gets the last word. Romance-coded beats: zero.
- No shoulder-proximity staging.
- No exchanged looks as punctuation.
- No protective gestures.
- No one carries or speaks "their" name.

**Mystery target.** Three separate stone facts (names, fragment, Mark) land on one wall, and the reader connects them. Meaning stays closed. The reader also registers that Voss's behavior doesn't match a man hearing this for the first time.

**Ending target.** The last beat is Aurelia's hand on the page, mid-statement. The final sentence is physical or a line of the record. It is not a thought, a question, a silence, or a door.

## 1B. Targeted Corrections and Verification Items

**Corrections (all within the locked architecture):**

1. **"Oldest first" scope.** Voss's instruction in S4 must be scoped to the Mark and the wall, meaning what each of them knew about those things and when. The Ch18 event is older than today's wall, so a global "oldest first" would put the Echo entry before the wall entry and break the ending. Scoping fixes this and also makes Aurelia's inclusion of the Ch18 event genuinely outside the letter of the instruction. That is what makes it her choice.
2. **Mark-history disclosure happens in S3, not in S2.** Aurelia and Aarav decide in S2 to report the wall. Whether she volunteers her earlier sightings is resolved in the room with Voss, when she could have stopped at "there's a mark by it" and doesn't. Do not stage a negotiation about it in S2.
3. **Lighting.** No flame, no glow, no light-conjuring, and no magic of any kind by anyone (Ch15's flame-as-light precedent is the temptation). Use existing or natural light only.
4. **No touch.** Neither Aurelia nor Aarav puts a hand on the stone at any point, and there are no palm-hovers (Ch11 pattern; Ch16 boundary).

**Verification items for the drafter (check the manuscript, don't assume):**

- (a) What Aarav already knows about her Ch6 rail sighting. If he doesn't know, do not have her tell him in S2. Her S3 statement to Voss becomes the first on-page surfacing, and Aarav's reaction belongs to Ch20.
- (b) That no earlier chapter (Ch1–18) uses Elyra or Varun by name on the page. The plan treats the names as new to the protagonists.
- (c) The Ch5/Ch6 Mark-geometry seam. Use only the safe formulation ("three straight strokes that don't quite close, with a gap"). Do not state gap position, orientation or proportions. Do not have anyone declare the marks "identical," only "the same kind."
- (d) Voss's existing physical baseline cues (Ch9/14/16). Reuse them at most once each, and never as the tell.

## 2. Scene-by-Scene Calibration

- **Aurelia's internal movement:** Control (evaluation prep, time pressure) → "this doesn't fit" → skilled rule-out (not heat) → recognition (the Mark) → confident reading (Neglect).
- **Aarav's function:** Absent. No mention, no "she thought of Aarav," no imagined commentary.
- **Voss's function:** Absent. His rules exert no pressure until S2.
- **Information revealed:**

  - What the Mark means.

  - Any link to the Echo.
- **Dialogue target:** Zero spoken lines. Do not have her read the names aloud (it turns them into incantation).
- **Physical-action target:** Moving with a purpose, taking the detour, slowing, stopping, crouching or tilting for a low line, changing angle for the light, comparing the damage to her memory of scorched material. Visual inspection only, no touching.
- **Relationship movement:** None.

  - Wall and damage: about 200.
  - Names: about 100.
  - Fragment: about 150.
  - Heat check: about 100.
  - Mark: about 100.
  - Frame: about 100.
- **Key drafting danger:**
  - The scene becomes an archaeology report.
  - Atmosphere substitutes for observation.

  - The first paragraph has no atmosphere and no mystery. It opens on an object or an action tied to her errand.
  - The errand is not a drill, a board, or a classroom.

  - The absence of context can't be shown to be accidental from what they can see.

- **Voss's function:** Absent. His standing terms (Ch16 "through me," Ch18 same-day) are the background pressure and are not restated.

  - Which frame is right.
  - Any statement of intent or blame.

- **Dialogue target:**

    2. Aarav names what the evidence can't show.
    3. Aurelia names what would change her mind.
    4. Aarav adds an alternative that looks the same from here.
    5. Aurelia accepts the uncertainty and won't delay.

  - Aarav becomes the primary analyst.
  - The argument turns into abstractions about institutions or memory.

  - Either character "wins."
  - The scene reads as clue → discuss → defer.

  - She reports because reporting is her decision. Aarav documents in the time it takes her to get there, and she does not wait for him to finish.
  - Aarav's Choice sentence must carry a "can't rule out" construction and no verbs of intent.
  - Do not stage them side by side at the wall. Use separate positions.
  - No shared "first version" callbacks and no Ch18 lines.
  - The scene opens with Aurelia mid-action (re-looking at something), not on his arrival.

## 3. Dialogue Calibration

**Aurelia sounds like:**
- She speaks in short, concrete, decisive sentences.
- She points at things ("that line," "here").
- She commits to a read, then says what would change it.
- Under pressure she gets plainer, not louder.
- With Voss she is direct and unpadded.
- She never uses "record," "process," or "protocol" in her own voice, except when quoting Voss.

**Aarav sounds like:**
- He is precise, small and conditional ("I can't tell from this").
- He says what the evidence supports, one point at a time.
- He asks at most one question at once.
- He doesn't use dry wit here. It would take attention from the argument.
- He never states a conclusion.

**Voss sounds like:**
- He gives complete, short sentences.
- His questions are procedural and ordered.
- His instructions are about the record, not about them.
- He uses no adjectives, no reassurance and no explanation.
- He never asks what she thinks it means.

**What stays implicit:**
- Why Voss doesn't ask about the names.
- What Aurelia thinks it means that he didn't.
- What Aarav thinks about the tell.
- Aurelia's disappointment or relief at Voss's non-reaction to her admission.

**Where silence and subtext help:**

- The wordless act of writing in S5.

**Where silence becomes the repetitive "Neither…" pattern:**
- Any beat where both characters react identically and inactively after something is said.
- A pause tag after Voss's instruction.
- A hush after her admission in S3.
- A hush after her observation in S4.
- A scene-ending silence in S2, S3 or S4.

If both characters must react, give each a different specific action. Do not tie them together with a shared inaction.

## 4. Voss Tell Calibration

**Primary tell: the unasked question.** Voss never asks about the names: not who they are, not whether the students recognize them, not what they mean.

- **What the reader sees:**
  - In S3, after Aurelia says the names, Voss's follow-ups are about place, timing, who else has seen the wall, and what has been touched or copied.
  - In S4, he verifies the transcript line by line against the wall, and his questions stay on physical facts.
  - At least one question should sit right next to the names (for example, about the line above or below them), so the omission is sharp.
  - Give him four to six questions across S3–S4, so a missing category is noticeable.
- **What Aurelia notices:** The question she came braced for (seeded in S2: she has an answer ready for "do you know them?") never arrives. She weighs it against everything else he did ask. She says it once, in a plain declarative sentence. She does not decode it.
- **What Aarav notices:** Nothing on the page. He shows no reaction, and Aurelia doesn't check his face. Ch20 owns his read of Voss's behavior.
- **What remains ambiguous:**
  - Whether Voss knows the names, knows what the wall is, or has some other reason not to ask.
  - Whether he is protecting the record from contamination, or acting on a prior reason.
  - His answer, one brief procedural sentence, neither confirms nor denies. It is not a lie.

**Do not add any second tell:** no pause, no stillness, no change in color or voice, no "for a moment," no looking away, no hand movement, no tightened jaw. The absence is the tell.

## 5. Aurelia Agency Audit

1. **She stops and reads (S1).** She has an errand and evaluation pressure and no reason to look. Her impatience with wasted time makes stopping a real cost. She also makes the heat rule-out from her own handling of scorched material (Ch12), and no one else in the story could make that call.
2. **She forms a reading and offers her own falsifier (S2).** She commits to Neglect from her own experience (she has never come this way herself). She also names what would change her mind, so she is willing to test why she might be wrong.
3. **She reports over "record first" and doesn't yield (S2).** This inverts Ch13, where she gave ground. She holds the decision and accepts the cost. It belongs to her because it depends on the acceptance of consequence, which is the arc turn this chapter sits on.
4. **She volunteers the Mark's history and says she should have reported it earlier (S3).** Voss did not ask and didn't have to. Her earlier omission (Ch6, and again at Ch14 after "everything") is now on her own account. It is a small, plain admission, not a confession.
5. **She includes the Ch18 event, outside the letter of the scoped instruction (S5).** Voss didn't demand it. She anchors it in her own Ch18 standard. It belongs to her because it is a keep-my-own-word decision, not compliance.

**Supporting beat (not load-bearing):** In S4 she states one plain observation to Voss. It must not read as a confrontation, and it is quiet agency, not an accusation.

## 8. Top 10 Drafting Red Flags

1. **Voss acknowledges anything.** No "those names," no "I know," no lie, no second tell, no explanation.
2. **Any identity, relationship, fate, or romance framing for Elyra and Varun.** This includes death, loss, date wording, banned adjectives, names assigned to characters, matched-pair staging, and mirroring.
3. **Inscription drift.** A complete sentence, any banned word (against, between, betrayed, responsible, guilty, belonged, either, first, ember, war/feud/quarrel), or tying the "fire" to Serpentfire or First Ember.
4. **Angular Mark defined.** No banned nouns (triangle, fourth, emblem, seal, signature, ancient mark, symbol of X), no geometry claims, no link to the fourth stone or the annex marks.
5. **Damage described as agentive or explained.** No erasure, deliberate removal, cover-up, or cause. No magic, no touching, no age judgments. Heat is ruled out visually and nothing more.
6. **Aarav probes, measures, touches, or becomes the analyst.** Also if Choice slides into a cover-up claim.
7. **Ch13 replay or a forced disclosure.** She yields, or Voss compels the Mark history. Also any new restriction on the students.
8. **Echo overreach.** Aarav's experience, the Ch8 comparison, speculation, a link to the wall, or any Ch18 fact altered.
9. **Ch20 spillover or a bad ending.** Statement matching, a post-scene conference about Voss, a "what do we do alone" debate, or ending on a question, reflection, silence or door.
10. **Voice and exposition drift.** Aurelia thinking like Aarav (cataloguing, bureaucratic), any explanatory paragraph about history, memorials or the Mark, and tic constructions.

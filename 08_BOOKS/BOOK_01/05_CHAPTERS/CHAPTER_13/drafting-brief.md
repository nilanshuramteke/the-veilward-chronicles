# Chapter 13 — Drafting Brief

> The primary Phase 4 writer-facing document. Read alongside `canon-constraints.md` and `scene-breakdown.md`. This brief translates approved architecture into execution instructions; it introduces no new story decisions.

## POV Discipline

Aurelia, close third person, past tense, throughout.

Full Aurelia interiority is expected and necessary — her recognition of the annex opportunity, her experience of fire as practical tool under pressure, and her reaction to the incomplete comparison and the Scene 4 decision are all required interiority beats.

Everything about Aarav must be inferred through his speech, physical action, and behavior, filtered through Aurelia's interpretation. No Aarav interiority.

## Narrative Texture

The chapter should feel:
- grounded and physical — an active worksite, not an institutional interior,
- ordinary before it's consequential — Scene 1 opens on tedium and complaint, not intrigue,
- self-authored rather than imposed — the risk and its cost belong to Aurelia and Aarav, not to an authority figure,
- incomplete rather than climactic — the chapter's payoff is a decision under uncertainty, not a resolution.

Avoid melodrama. The annex should read as an ordinary, aging part of the Academy undergoing ordinary renovation — not a hidden or forbidden space.

## Dev

Give him a real scene, not a compressed information delivery — the editorial review has flagged the supporting-cast gap as urgent, and this is the last chapter before the midpoint where it can land. He needs his own specific complaint, his own voice, and his own reason for being where he is, established before he mentions the break routine. Keep the mention of the propped door incidental — part of his complaint about the tedium, not information offered as a favor. He does not know why Aurelia is interested and does not ask beyond ordinary curiosity. He does not appear again after Scene 1.

## The Eastern Annex

Make it feel physically real through clutter, scaffolding, displaced paneling, and the ordinary mess of an active worksite — not through description blocks. The discrepancy the protagonists find is a record-vs-condition gap, not an impossible space. No secret chamber, no magical infrastructure.

## The Three-Day Clock and the Break Window

Keep clock references sparse — established clearly once in Scene 1, felt rather than counted through the chapter. The break window itself (Scenes 2-3) is the chapter's actual ticking element; do not layer a second, redundant countdown on top of it. The crew's return in Scene 3 should be heard — voices, equipment, footsteps — and should read as ordinary and on-schedule, not as a close call. The dramatic beat is stopping short, not escaping.

## The Annex Marks

Two marks, spatially distinct, sharing enough in kind to suggest recurrence. Keep the physical description sensory and concrete (texture, placement, what surrounds them) rather than analytical — let Aurelia and Aarav's attempted comparison happen through observation and dialogue, not through exposition that explains what the marks mean. Neither character reaches a conclusion. No instrument, measurement, or magical confirmation may be used on either mark.

## Aurelia

Her fire is used exactly as already established — practical light and warmth, nothing more. Her instinct to disclose immediately in Scene 4 must be given real weight on the page; the joint decision only works dramatically if her position was a genuine pull in the other direction, not a formality to be overcome.

## Aarav

His Scene 4 reasoning must explicitly recall Chapter 12 — the isolated sighting that cost them the Archive and produced no answers. Frame his position as "a complete account has a better chance of being acted on than another fragment," never as "we should keep this quiet." His Naga sensitivity in Scene 2 stays inconclusive and unclassified — no comparison to any named phenomenon.

## Relationship

Keep rivalry and friction present in Scene 1's negotiation. Scenes 2-3 should read as synchronized, wordless cooperation under shared pressure. Scene 4 is the chapter's relationship payoff — a decision authored by both, arrived at through Aarav's reasoning and Aurelia's instinct together. If the one permitted romance-temperature beat is used, keep it brief, physical, and subordinate to the actual action (working in close quarters by firelight) — never named, never mutual in the moment, and never built from a "noticed something → didn't examine why" construction.

## Prose Watchlist

Carried forward from prior chapter audits, with two items specifically corroborated by `13_REVIEWS_AND_AUDITS/book-01-review.md`'s own findings on the draft:

- **Cognitive-storage metaphors** — "filed it away," "set aside," "stored it," "tucked away," "shelved it," "kept it in the back of the mind." The editorial review names "filed it" as an existing repeated tic in the manuscript — treat this as an active risk, not a hypothetical one.
- **Refusal-to-introspect constructions** — "didn't examine why," "didn't ask why," "didn't look too closely at why," "chose not to think about it," "refused to name it." Flagged after two appearances in Chapter 12; do not substitute a synonym performing the same rhetorical function.
- Repeated "the way [subject][verb]" constructions.
- Mirrored/triple negation ("not X, not Y, but Z").
- Trailing "which..." clauses.
- Clustering of "already."
- Symmetrical dialogue.
- Abstract emotional labels without physical grounding.
- Excessive fire metaphor for Aurelia.
- Generic mystery language ("ominous," "strange," "impossible," "watching," "waiting," "alive").
- The observation → pushback → jab → silence → exit template — the review credits Chapters 11 and 12 with breaking it two chapters running; Chapter 13's Scene 4 should continue that trend through joint reasoning rather than reverting to it.

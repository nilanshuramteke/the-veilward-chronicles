## Relationship Ledger

**Starting state:** Stage 2 — Forced Allies / Rival Cooperation, beginning edge of Stage 3 — Respect (inherited from Ch12, tested but not advanced).

**Intended movement:** shared risk → joint choice → shared consequence.

**Allowed:** cooperative physical action without negotiation (Scenes 2-3); a real, weighed negotiation in Scene 1; a jointly-authored decision in Scene 4 where neither party simply yields; at most one small, unforced, behavior-only romance-temperature beat.

**Forbidden:** confession, explicit attraction, emotional intimacy, friendship declaration, sudden trust, any romance-stage advancement, naming the relationship shift in dialogue or interiority, a repeat of Ch11's validation structure or Ch12's disagreement-then-restraint structure.

## Clock / Consequence Ledger

**Clock begins:** Scene 1 — the eastern annex's pre-existing, ordinary three-day renovation/sealing schedule, unrelated in origin to the mystery.

**What causes the window:** ordinary Academy maintenance scheduling, already in motion before the protagonists learn of it.

**What opportunity it creates:** the work crew's daily break leaves the annex briefly unsupervised, known to Dev firsthand.

**What becomes impossible after it closes:** further physical examination of the specific exposed section — the crew's next phase is resealing exactly that area, making this the protagonists' one viable window, not a repeatable one.

**How Scene 3 uses the shorter work window:** the daily break itself is the chapter's actual ticking element; it ends the investigation mid-comparison.

**How the three-day clock carries forward:** two days remain at chapter's end; not dramatized further, but reinforces that the Scene 4 decision is made under a genuinely closing set of options, not idle deliberation.

**Chapter 14 inheritance:** the annex thread is now closed — no further access is possible. Chapter 14 opens with three named, unresolved threads held in reserve by mutual choice, and unspoken pressure not to let the delay become permanent by default.

## Supporting-Cast Ledger

**Why Dev matters:** without his firsthand, incidental knowledge of the crew's break routine, Aurelia and Aarav have no plausible way to know the opportunity exists at all — he is the sole source of the information that makes the chapter's engine possible. He is not a lore source, a secret investigator, or a keyholder — he grants nothing and understands nothing about the mystery. His inclusion is not a decorative response to the editorial review; removing him removes the chapter's opportunity entirely.

Mira and Lyra are correctly absent — their absence does not undermine the supporting-cast requirement, since Dev alone is genuinely load-bearing this chapter.

## Reader-Condition Test

- **Supporting cast:** Dev materially matters — the opportunity does not exist without him.
- **Real clock:** the three-day renovation window is ordinary, pre-scheduled, and produces a genuinely irreversible consequence.
- **Physical action:** Scenes 2-3 involve real movement through a cluttered, active worksite — distinct in kind from Chapters 9–12's investigation-and-discussion shape.
- **World texture:** ordinary Academy maintenance, worksite routine, and student obligation are delivered through task, not exposition.
- **Mystery progression:** the annex pattern sharpens the mystery's question without resolving or over-explaining it.
- **Structural variety:** the engine (ordinary obligation → opportunity → incomplete discovery → self-authored consequence) does not repeat Ch11's validation engine or Ch12's institutional-consequence engine.
- **Relationship:** movement is shared risk → joint choice, distinct from both Ch11 and Ch12.

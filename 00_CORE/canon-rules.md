# Canon Rules — The Veilward Chronicles

> **Document Type:** Canon Governance and Continuity Framework  
> **Status:** ACTIVE  
> **Scope:** Entire Series and Shared Universe  
> **Purpose:** Define how story facts become canon, how canon is updated, how contradictions are resolved, and how all repository documents should remain internally consistent.

---

# 1. Purpose of This Document

The Veilward Chronicles is being developed through multiple interconnected markdown files.

As the series grows, those files will contain information about:

- The world.
- Mythical species.
- Characters.
- Magic.
- History.
- Institutions.
- Relationships.
- Individual books.
- Chapter-level events.

Without a continuity system, information can gradually become inconsistent.

A character may have two different ages.

A magical rule may change without explanation.

A historical event may be described differently across files.

A future book outline may contradict an established character arc.

This document defines how such situations are handled.

## Core Principle

> **Canon is intentional. Nothing becomes permanent simply because it was written once.**

---

# 2. What Is Canon?

Canon refers to information officially accepted as true within The Veilward Chronicles universe.

Canon includes established facts about:

- Characters.
- Events.
- History.
- Locations.
- Species.
- Magic.
- Institutions.
- Relationships.
- Timeline.

Canon exists at different levels of certainty.

Not every idea currently written in the repository has the same authority.

---

# 3. Canon Status Levels

Every important story concept should eventually belong to one of the following categories.

## CONFIRMED CANON

Information that has been intentionally approved as part of the established universe.

Confirmed Canon should be treated as true unless officially revised.

Examples:

- Aurelia is a female Phoenix.
- Aarav is a male Naga.
- The central romance follows Enemies → Friends → Lovers.
- Most mythical creatures have one fixed Mortal Form.
- True shapeshifters can assume multiple appearances.
- The Veilward Academy trains young mythical creatures.
- The Veilwardens protect the hidden and human worlds.

---

## PROVISIONAL CANON

Information currently accepted for development purposes but still open to revision.

Provisional Canon may be changed when:

- A stronger idea is developed.
- A contradiction is discovered.
- A later worldbuilding decision requires adjustment.
- The series structure changes.

Provisional Canon should still be respected while active.

Do not casually contradict it.

---

## OPEN QUESTION

An important area intentionally left undefined.

Open Questions should not be treated as missing work that must be solved immediately.

Some questions need to remain open until:

- The relevant book is developed.
- More worldbuilding is completed.
- A mystery requires delayed revelation.

Example:

> What is the complete origin of the Outer Dark?

Until formally established, the answer remains open.

---

## NON-CANON IDEA

An idea explored during brainstorming but not adopted into the official universe.

Non-Canon Ideas may include:

- Rejected plotlines.
- Alternative character arcs.
- Experimental concepts.
- Early drafts.

Non-Canon Ideas must not silently re-enter the story as established facts.

---

## RETCONNED

Information that was previously considered canon but has been officially replaced.

The old information should remain documented for continuity history but must no longer be treated as active canon.

---

# 4. Canon Authority Hierarchy

When two files contain conflicting information, use the following hierarchy.

## Level 1: Explicit Canon Decisions

The most recent deliberate decision establishing or revising canon.

This includes clearly marked canon updates.

---

## Level 2: Core Canon Documents

Documents defining the highest-level series foundation.

Examples:

- `series-overview.md`
- `core-premise.md`
- `themes.md`
- `series-pillars.md`
- `canon-rules.md`

---

## Level 3: Dedicated World and Character Files

Documents specifically responsible for a subject.

Examples:

- Character profiles.
- Species files.
- Magic system files.
- Academy files.
- Historical timeline files.

A dedicated character file has authority over general character details.

A dedicated magic file has authority over general magic mechanics.

---

## Level 4: Book Blueprints

Individual book documents define how established canon appears within that book.

They may add new canon.

However, they must not casually contradict higher-level canon.

---

## Level 5: Chapter Blueprints

Chapter-level files define the detailed execution of events.

They must remain consistent with:

- The book blueprint.
- Character canon.
- World canon.
- Series canon.

---

## Level 6: Draft Manuscripts

Manuscripts are execution documents.

A manuscript draft may contain:

- Errors.
- Temporary decisions.
- Unapproved changes.

Draft text does not automatically override the canon repository.

---

Every major category should eventually have a primary source of truth.

Examples:

| Category | Primary Canon Location |

| Series Foundation | `00_CORE/` |
| World Rules | `01_WORLD/` |
| Mythical Species | `02_SPECIES/` |

A fact should ideally have one authoritative home.

Other files may summarize it.

They should not create competing versions.

# 6. The Canon Update Process

When a new idea is introduced, it should follow a deliberate process.

## Step 1: Proposal

The idea is explored.

At this stage, it is not automatically canon.

---

## Step 2: Compatibility Check

Ask:

- Does this contradict existing canon?
- Does this improve the story?
- Does this create unintended consequences?
- Does it affect future books?
- Does it require changes elsewhere?

---

## Step 3: Canon Decision

The idea becomes one of:

- Confirmed Canon.
- Provisional Canon.
- Open Question.
- Non-Canon.

---

## Step 4: Update the Primary Source

The appropriate authoritative document should be updated.

Do not rely on scattered mentions.

---

## Step 5: Update Dependent Files

If the decision affects:

- Characters.
- Magic.
- History.
- Book blueprints.

those files should also be reviewed.

---

## Step 6: Record the Change

Significant canon changes should be recorded in the Canon Change Log.

---

# 7. Confirmed Canon Must Not Be Casually Changed

Confirmed Canon may still be revised.

However, revisions require a deliberate decision.

Before changing Confirmed Canon, ask:

### Why is the change necessary?

### What existing material does it affect?

### Does the new version improve the story?

### Can the desired improvement be achieved without changing canon?

### What future plotlines depend on the original information?

A change should not happen simply because a new idea sounds interesting.

---

# 8. The Contradiction Resolution Protocol

When a contradiction is discovered, do not immediately choose whichever version appears most recently.

Follow this process.

## Step 1: Identify the Contradiction

Write the two conflicting statements clearly.

Example:

```text
Statement A:
Aurelia discovers Serpentfire during Book I.

Statement B:
Aurelia first encounters Serpentfire before entering the Academy.
```

---

## Step 2: Identify the Sources

Determine:

- Which files contain the statements?
- What is the canon authority level of each file?
- Which statement was deliberate?
- Which may be an outdated draft?

---

## Step 3: Determine the Intended Canon

Review:

- Series themes.
- Core premise.
- Character arcs.
- Book structure.

Choose the version that best supports the established story.

---

## Step 4: Make an Explicit Decision

The contradiction must be resolved intentionally.

Do not leave both versions active.

---

## Step 5: Update All Relevant Files

The repository should contain one active version.

---

## Step 6: Record the Resolution

If the contradiction affects important canon, add it to the Canon Change Log.

---

# 9. No Silent Retcons

A significant change to established canon must never happen silently.

If a fact changes, the repository should make it possible to understand:

- What changed.
- Why it changed.
- Which version is active.

This does not mean the final published story needs to mention the change.

This is an internal development rule.

## Core Principle

> **The audience may never see the revision process. The author must still understand it.**

---

# 10. The Canon Change Log

Major canon changes should be recorded in a dedicated section or file.

Recommended format:

```markdown
## Canon Change: [Title]

**Date:** YYYY-MM-DD

**Previous Canon:**
[Previous information]

**New Canon:**
[Updated information]

**Reason:**
[Why the change was made]

**Affected Files:**
- file-name.md
- file-name.md

**Status:**
Active
```

A dedicated file may later be created:

`CANON-CHANGELOG.md`

---

# 11. The Rule of Explicit Uncertainty

If something is unknown, mark it as unknown.

Do not invent temporary answers simply to make a document feel complete.

Use labels such as:

- `OPEN QUESTION`
- `TO BE DEVELOPED`
- `PROVISIONAL`
- `INTENTIONALLY UNDEFINED`

This is especially important for:

- The Outer Dark.
- Ancient history.
- Future book revelations.
- Secret identities.
- Long-term antagonists.

## Core Principle

> **Undefined is better than accidentally contradictory.**

---

# 12. Mystery Information and Canon

A mystery can have a confirmed answer even when that answer is hidden from the reader.

Therefore, distinguish between:

## AUTHOR KNOWLEDGE

The truth known to the series development system.

## CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE

What a particular character currently knows.

## READER KNOWLEDGE

What the audience has been shown.

These three levels must never be confused.

Example:

```text
AUTHOR KNOWLEDGE:
The identity of a historical figure is known.

CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE:
Aurelia suspects several possibilities.

READER KNOWLEDGE:
The reader has only seen clues.
```

---

# 13. The Knowledge Layer Rule

For important mysteries, documents should eventually track:

```text
TRUTH
   ↓
Who knows the truth?
   ↓
What do characters believe?
   ↓
What has the reader learned?
```

This prevents:

- Accidental spoilers.
- Characters knowing things they should not know.
- Repeated mystery explanations.
- Inconsistent reactions.

---

# 14. Character Canon Rules

Each major character should eventually have a dedicated profile.

Character facts should include:

- Name.
- Species.
- Age.
- Appearance.
- Mortal Form.
- Personality.
- History.
- Family.
- Abilities.
- Weaknesses.
- Goals.
- Fears.
- Relationships.
- Character arc.

Once confirmed, important details should remain consistent.

---

## Character Change Rule

Characters may change emotionally.

They should not randomly change personality.

If a character behaves unexpectedly, the story must provide a reason.

Example:

Bad continuity:

> Aarav suddenly becomes reckless because the plot requires a fight.

Better development:

> Aarav gradually learns to take necessary risks and eventually makes a calculated decision that appears reckless but is consistent with his growth.

## Core Principle

> **Character growth changes behavior through experience, not convenience.**

---

# 15. Relationship Canon Rules

Relationships must have timelines.

Important events should eventually track:

- First meeting.
- First conflict.
- First trust.
- First vulnerability.
- Major disagreements.
- Friendship milestones.
- Romantic realization.
- Relationship commitment.

The central romance must respect its established progression.

Aurelia and Aarav should not emotionally jump ahead because a later scene requires romance.

---

# 16. Magic Canon Rules

Every significant magical system must eventually define:

- What it can do.
- What it cannot do.
- Costs.
- Limitations.
- Risks.
- Known exceptions.

An exception must never silently destroy the rule.

If an exception exists, ask:

> Why is this exception possible?

Serpentfire should have its own dedicated canon documentation.

---

# 17. Worldbuilding Canon Rules

Worldbuilding must remain internally consistent.

When adding:

- Locations.
- Communities.
- Institutions.
- Species.

ask:

- Where is it located?
- How does it interact with humanity?
- How does the Veil affect it?
- Who governs it?
- What history connects it to the wider world?

The answer may initially be unknown.

If so, mark it as an Open Question.

---

# 18. Timeline Canon Rules

The timeline is one of the most important continuity systems.

Every major event should eventually have:

- Relative placement.
- Cause.
- Consequence.

Important timeline categories include:

- Ancient history.
- Creation or development of the Veil.
- Historical events involving Elyra and Varun.
- The founding and evolution of the Academy.
- Events before Book I.
- Events during each book.

Avoid vague timeline statements when precise placement is required.

---

# 19. Book Canon Rules

Each book blueprint should define:

- Starting state.
- Ending state.
- Major character changes.
- Relationship progression.
- Mystery progression.
- Major revelations.
- Primary conflict.
- Consequences for the next book.

The ending state of one book becomes the starting canon of the next.

## Core Principle

> **Books must connect through consequences, not merely chronology.**

---

# 20. Chapter Canon Rules

A chapter blueprint must not contradict its book blueprint.

Each chapter should know:

- When it occurs.
- Whose perspective is used.
- What changes.
- What characters know.
- What new information is revealed.

A chapter should never introduce a major permanent fact without reviewing its effect on the wider canon.

---

# 21. Drafting Canon Rules

When writing a chapter or manuscript:

## The writer may:

- Add small sensory details.
- Expand dialogue.
- Develop atmosphere.
- Add minor supporting moments.

## The writer must not:

- Change major character history.
- Invent major powers.
- Alter relationship progression.
- Reveal mysteries early.
- Kill major characters without canon approval.
- Change established timeline events.

If a better idea appears during drafting:

> Record it as a proposal.

Do not silently rewrite the canon.

---

# 22. The Canon Review Before Writing

Before generating or drafting a major chapter, review:

### Series Context

- Core premise.
- Themes.
- Series pillars.

### Character Context

- Relevant character profiles.
- Relationship status.
- Emotional state.

### Book Context

- Current book blueprint.
- Previous chapter consequences.
- Upcoming milestones.

### Mystery Context

- What is true?
- What does the POV character know?
- What should the reader know?

---

# 23. The Canon Review After Writing

After drafting a chapter, check:

## Character

- Did anyone behave inconsistently?
- Did emotional reactions make sense?

## Timeline

- Does the chapter occur in the correct sequence?

## Knowledge

- Did anyone know information they should not know?

## Magic

- Did any ability contradict established rules?

## Relationships

- Did the emotional progression move too quickly?

## Mystery

- Was anything revealed too early?

# 25. The Provisional-to-Confirmed Rule

Provisional information should become Confirmed Canon only when:

1. It supports the wider series.
2. Its consequences are understood.
3. It does not create contradictions.
4. The author intentionally approves it.

Do not automatically confirm something because it has existed in the repository for a long time.

---

# 26. The Open Question Protection Rule

Do not accidentally answer an Open Question inside:

- Dialogue.
- Character backstory.
- Worldbuilding notes.
- Draft chapters.

An unanswered mystery may be intentionally unanswered.

Before establishing new information, check whether the topic is marked as:

> OPEN QUESTION.

---

# 27. The Retcon Rule

A retcon should only occur when one of the following is true:

- A contradiction must be resolved.
- A better structure significantly improves the story.
- Earlier development created an unsustainable future problem.
- New information requires a deliberate reinterpretation.

Retcons should not occur simply because:

> A new idea feels more exciting.

---

# 28. Soft Retcons and Hard Retcons

## Soft Retcon

A clarification that adds information without directly contradicting earlier canon.

Example:

Earlier:

> The Veil protects the human world.

Later clarification:

> The Veil protects the human world through multiple interconnected magical systems.

Both statements can remain true.

---

## Hard Retcon

A previous fact is replaced.

Example:

Previous Canon:

> Serpentfire was discovered in the ancient era.

New Canon:

> Serpentfire existed before the ancient era but was rediscovered later.

The earlier statement may need formal replacement.

Hard Retcons require documentation.

---

# 29. The Canon Stability Principle

Not every detail deserves the same level of protection.

## Highly Stable Canon

Changing these would significantly affect the series.

Examples:

- Aurelia is a Phoenix.
- Aarav is a Naga.
- Their romance progression.
- Fixed Mortal Forms.
- True shapeshifter distinction.
- The existence of the Veil.
- The Veilward Academy.
- The hopeful ending promise.

---

## Moderately Stable Canon

Important but potentially revisable.

Examples:

- Specific Academy structures.
- Supporting character details.
- Exact historical dates.

---

## Flexible Development Areas

Ideas expected to evolve.

Examples:

- Book titles.
- Minor locations.
- Some side plots.
- Minor characters.

# 30. Current Confirmed Canon

The following information is currently established as Confirmed Canon.

## Series

- Series title: **The Veilward Chronicles**.
- Primary genre: Mythological Fantasy.
- The series includes romance, academy fantasy, mystery, adventure, and coming-of-age elements.
- The current series structure is planned as a trilogy.

---

## World

- Humanity does not generally know mythical creatures exist.
- Mythical creatures exist in a hidden world alongside humanity.
- The Veil conceals and protects the hidden mythical world and human world.
- The Veil has deeper history and complexity that will be revealed gradually.
- The Outer Dark represents a major long-term supernatural threat.

---

## Protagonists

- Aurelia is female.
- Aurelia is a Phoenix.
- Aarav is male.
- Aarav is a Naga.
- Aurelia and Aarav are the primary protagonists.
- They are the emotional center of the series.

---

## Romance

- Aurelia and Aarav begin in conflict and rivalry.
- Their relationship develops gradually.
- Their relationship follows the overall progression:

```text
Enemies
→ Friends
→ Lovers
```

- Friendship is essential before romance.
- Their love is based on choice.
- Magic and destiny must not remove their free will.
- Their relationship receives a satisfying and hopeful resolution.

---

## Forms and Shapeshifting

- Mythical creatures possess True Forms.
- Most mythical creatures also possess one fixed Mortal Form.
- Ordinary mythical creatures cannot freely change their Mortal Form into different faces or identities.
- True shapeshifters can assume multiple forms and appearances.
- True shapeshifters are distinct from ordinary mythical creatures using Mortal Forms.

---

## Institutions

- The Veilward Academy exists as a hidden institution.
- The Academy trains young mythical creatures.
- The Academy teaches control, responsibility, honor, history, and the way of living within the hidden world.
- The Veilwardens protect the Veil, the hidden world, and humanity.

---

## Mystery

- Serpentfire exists.
- Serpentfire emerges through an unusual interaction involving Aurelia and Aarav's magic.
- Its true nature is initially unknown.
- Serpentfire is connected to forgotten history.
- Elyra is an important historical Phoenix.
- Varun is an important historical Naga.
- Their history is incomplete or deliberately erased.
- The truth surrounding their story is a major series mystery.

---

## Ending Promise

- The central mystery will receive meaningful resolution.
- The larger threat from the Outer Dark will be addressed.
- Aurelia and Aarav will experience complete emotional arcs.
- Their relationship will end positively and satisfyingly.
- The overall emotional conclusion of the series is hopeful.

# 33. Current Canon Boundaries

The following ideas must not be introduced without deliberate canon development.

- New forms of unlimited shapeshifting.
- Powers that eliminate magical consequences.
- Prophecies forcing Aurelia and Aarav to become romantically involved.
- Sudden changes to the hidden-world secrecy structure.
- Major historical revelations about Elyra and Varun.
- Full explanations of the Outer Dark.
- Major permanent changes to the protagonists' species or identities.
- A tragic ending that violates the established hopeful ending promise.

---

# 34. Canon Decision Checklist

Before confirming a new major idea, ask:

### Consistency

Does it contradict existing canon?

### Story

Does it improve the narrative?

### Character

Does it support meaningful character development?

### Theme

Does it fit the thematic foundation?

### Consequence

What else changes because this becomes true?

### Future

Does it create problems for later books?

### Necessity

Is this information needed now?

---

# 35. The Final Canon Principle

Canon exists to protect creative freedom, not destroy it.

A well-organized canon system allows the series to grow confidently.

The goal is not to prevent new ideas.

The goal is to ensure that good ideas have a place.

Before changing the world, understand what the change affects.

Before establishing a new truth, understand the old one.

Before revealing a mystery, understand who already knows the answer.

And always remember:

> **Consistency makes the world believable.**

> **Consequences make the story meaningful.**

> **Choice keeps the characters alive.**

---

# 36. Repository Operating Rule

For all future development of The Veilward Chronicles:

> **When a new idea conflicts with existing canon, do not silently overwrite the old idea.**

Instead:

1. Identify the conflict.
2. Determine the canon authority.
3. Decide intentionally.
4. Update the primary source.
5. Update dependent files.
6. Record significant changes.

This rule applies to:

- Worldbuilding.
- Characters.
- Magic.
- History.
- Relationships.
- Book plots.
- Chapter events.
